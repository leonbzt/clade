# Clade's shape: lineages, island runs and a slot board (29 Oct)

From the planning chat of 28–29 Oct. It replaces the first draft of this file (one lineage on one island).
**An AI plan built on your answers; your calls are in P24 ([systems.md](systems.md)).** Nothing moves into the
brief until you move it.

## 0. In short

- **Home is the garden.** The sim turns each region into a **slot board**: the roles its ecosystem has (ground
  cover, moss grazer, hexapod hunter…), how big each one is, and who holds it. You win slots; the sim decides.
- **Lineages are your characters.** One to three or four, each with a class (its clade), stats, abilities and a
  level that grow across runs. You grow one thing the whole time.
- **Runs happen on young islands** of the archipelago. You land a lineage with one or two helpers, play its
  generations (broods, the heir's spot, keys, seasons, blooms) and bring the adapted line home as a new species.
- **Coming home is the payoff.** You release the line into a region and it has to take a slot from its holder.
  The lines you hold feed each other through the food web.
- **The world moves.** Seasons, succession and events resize slots, so either you already have the answer (a gift)
  or you want it (a challenge).
- **Rare things are real data** (forms, keys, morphs, seeded islands), so they're worth finding and talking about.

*Real:* this is how archipelagos work. Darwin's finches spread between the Galápagos islands and adapted on each.

## 1. Principles to hold true

1. **The sim decides; the game names it.** Slots, holders, gains and forecasts come from the sim's real numbers.
   Authoring sets the vocabulary (roles, names), the pacing (which roles exist when) and the tuning, never results.
2. **You grow one thing the whole time.** A lineage is a character that keeps its level and identity.
3. **Runs change the line; home pays for it.** Adapting happens on the run island; the reward and the interaction
   happen at home.
4. **Everything you add helps something else.** Your lines boost each other through the food web, and the board
   shows the links.
5. **Genes appear and spread by themselves; you pick from offers; designing is scarce.** Mutation and spread on
   their own stay at the heart, at home and on runs (you like this a lot). Broods add choices on top and never
   replace it; full control (nudges, the nursery) costs something limited.
6. **Show simple, reveal depth as progress.** Deep mechanics under a simple surface. The reasons you're shown are
   always true, and they are the forecast.
7. **The world moves.** Change is how answers become gifts and wants become challenges.
8. **Rare means rare in the data**, not a label: findable, explainable, shareable.
9. **Fair to the clock, cheap to compute.** Results never depend on where the camera is. One engine runs every
   island.
10. **Every system starts as its smallest version** and grows only when a playtest asks for it.

## 2. The pieces

### Home and the slot board
- **Regions:** named areas from the generator (landmarks and terrain first; wind and height later). A bigger home
  island comes later, or grows by itself (eruptions add land).
- **Roles:** a short authored list, mostly generated from data: a producer role per plant layer, an eater role per
  food tag, a hunter role per prey tag, plus fixer and decomposer. A new food tag makes a new role by itself.
- **A slot is a region plus a role.** The sim gives it:
  - **size:** what that role has on offer there (ground in that layer, food per minute);
  - **use:** how much of it is taken;
  - **holder:** the species with the biggest share, and its tier;
  - **trend**, and **fed by** (the slots that feed it).
- Averaged over a few minutes, so it reads calmly. An open slot nobody can fill yet is a goal, not a bug.
- **On the map, not only in a panel:** each region shows its name and a row of role icons (yours, wild, open).
  Long term the board may grow into a big table, and that's fine, as long as it also lives on the map.
- **Your gains from a slot:** size × your share × how well your build fits, scaled per region so tuning stays
  easy. Fit matters most within a band, with diminishing returns, so no single build is a must.
- **The garden keeps evolving.** Rare variants turn up at home; one can found a new lineage or ride along on a run.
- **Wild holders** (arrivals, locals, lines that drifted) are rivals in the sim for now. Winning them over can
  come later.

### Lineages
- **A lineage is a character:** a name, a class (its clade), kingdom stats, abilities, its current form, and a
  family tree of the species it has made.
- **Levels from runs:** XP for generations, the fit it reached, keys and finds. Levels give perks: a fourth brood
  card, more reach, an ability slot, later stat points.
- **Each run starts from one of its descendants**, so a lineage can branch (take the Dry Moss, or the Wet one).
- **Slots for lineages:** one at the start, a second early, three or four at most.
- **Keys** (class changes) are found on runs: the recipe met, then the key turning up in a brood.

### Runs: how they play
1. **Choose.** The archipelago map offers 2–3 young islands, each with a theme from its seed (dry, wet, salt, ash,
   caves), a season length and a hint of its locals. You pick the island, a lineage and its starting form, a target
   slot at home (optional), and one or two **helpers** from your roster: full species (say a nitrogen fixer that builds
   the ground for your moss), and sometimes other things that give a boost.
2. **Land.** Place the founder and the helpers: the run's big placement.
3. **Generations.** The hero (a patch or a family) has a brood of three: *Steady* (a small gain), *Cross* (a gene
   from a variant nearby), *Wild* (a mutation with a trade-off). You pick the heir and its spot. Your line also
   mutates by itself as it spreads; those variants are the Cross material and the finds.
4. **Seasons and timers.** A wet window helps heirs settle, a dry spell tests them, and blooms ripen (collect
   seeds and evolution points within the window; later this is automatic). An event now and then.
5. **Locals.** Each island has a few wild species: competition, and things to discover.
6. **The end:** the island's clock (a number of seasons), your call ("bring it home", after a minimum), or the line
   dies. A run fits one active session, or a few days of idling with standing orders.
7. **Home.** The adapted line (its main form and its shown variants) becomes a new species. You place one founder
   (more with upgrades) in a region and see the **forecast**: the reasons it should win or lose against the holder
   ("fits 0.8 vs 0.6 · Hold 6 vs 4 · a dry season in 3 min"). Then the sim decides.
- **The island's theme is the point:** to fill the dry lee slot at home, run your line on a dry island.
- **Finished islands** stay on the map as outposts with a small trickle by theme (seeds or biomass). Going back
  isn't needed.

### The nursery (breeding, kept scarce)
- Costs **seeds** (from blooms and outposts). Cross two genomes from your roster or home variants, get a brood of
  three, keep one as the founder for a next run. This is where "I made this" lives.

### The moving world (home)
- **Seasons** with a forecast: wet and dry windows you can time a release to.
- **Succession** opens roles over time (litter builds up: a decomposer slot; a herb layer: herb grazers).
- **Events:** a storm on the windward coast, a vent flare, an invader. Some announced, some sudden.

### Discovery and rarity
- **Forms:** named gene combinations with an extra effect (today's combos, grown up). Some are listed, some hinted
  (a silhouette and a clue in the codex), some hidden. "New form: Ironcap Moss."
- **Keys:** the class changes.
- **Morphs:** rare looks with little or no effect: a colour, a pattern, a left-coiled snail (real, and very rare).
  Cheap to make, and the kind of thing people post.
- **Locals:** rare natives on run islands.
- **The codex** (a collection log): species, forms, morphs, locals, firsts.
- **Tiers come from the odds:** a script works out how unlikely each form and morph is from the mutation numbers,
  so "Legendary" is true.
- **Sharing without a server:** islands come from seeds ("seed 4172 has a Legendary beetle on the north cape"),
  and a genome code shows a species (view-only at first).

### What you see first
- At first: fit as good / okay / poor, each slot's size and holder, brood cards in words.
- **Lenses unlock as progress:** the layer views (water, light, richness), stat details, the full "why", the
  forecast's numbers. Learning the island is the naturalist's progression.
- Hard to predict is fine, as long as the reason afterwards ("why it lost") is honest.

## 3. Loops

- **Moment:** pick the young that can live in the land ahead, and see whether it holds.
- **Check-in (5 min):** spend banked time, collect blooms, read the board (what changed, what opened), queue a
  release or a run.
- **Session (30–60 min):** play a run from landing to home, or finish one and win a slot with it.
- **Long term:** every region of home thriving (§4), the tree climbing class by class, the archipelago filling up.

**Check-in:** a dry season hit, two of your holders fit badly, the hunter slot on the lee shore opened, and a bloom
on your run island gives two seeds.
**Session:** the moss run ends with a Rare Dry Moss and an odd local beetle. You release the moss on the lee shore
in the wet window (forecast 70%); it takes the ground cover, the grazer slot grows and your snails gain. The beetle
goes into the nursery, then on a run aimed at the hunter slot.

## 4. Economy and the long target

| | From | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Biomass** | your held slots (size × share × fit) | lineage slots, helpers, run upgrades, the banked-time cap, lenses, recipe reveals |
| **Evolution points** | per lineage, on runs: new ground, births, blooms | rerolls, nudges, later stat points |
| **Seeds** | blooms, outposts | the nursery |
| **Levels** | XP from runs | lineage perks |

- **The long target is the island's climax.** When every region of home thrives (a short checklist read from its
  board, like Stardew's bundles), an era closes with a capstone clade. Era 1: pioneers → vascular plants. Era 2:
  forest → seed plants. Era 3: animals → flight, or the first vertebrates coming ashore. The next era changes the
  island (new land, new roles) and the archipelago grows; lineages carry over. Deep-time runs and a forever island
  come after that.

## 5. Time and compute

- **Each island has its own clock.** The one you look at runs live. The others run in the background at a coarse
  step, and stable regions sleep (like a physics engine's resting bodies) until something nearby changes.
- **Away time banks** (capped; the cap is an upgrade). On return you see a summary and spend banked time to fast-
  forward an island or a run, or spread it automatically. Fast-forward runs in a web worker with a progress bar.
- **Fair to the clock:** catch-up uses fixed coarse steps, so results don't depend on the camera or on when you look.
- **Lazy regions** (your idea: a region computed only when you open it) are the next step if a bigger home island
  needs it. Borders then trade only through their edge tiles. Not before.
- **Saves:** versioned, with an export string, from the slice that adds banked time.

## 6. Genes and stats: the place to rework them (genome v2)

Today's genes carry slices 11–12. The rework comes once the first runs show what matters. Five parts, all data:
1. **Niche genes:** two directions that move need bands (Dry/Wet, Shade/Sun; animals: Dry/Wet, and Diet on a
   food graph).
2. **Stats:** Spread, Hold, Endure, Grow, plus Pace (fast–slow, the Metabolism gene). Every point costs upkeep
   that the land pays, so rich and poor regions favour different builds. Replaces Lean/Rich and Wind/Tight.
3. **Abilities:** traits and keys as chips with an up and a down.
4. **Forms:** named combinations with an extra effect, some hidden.
5. **Morphs:** looks.
- Genes act on the shared stats; a table per kingdom maps those to sim numbers. A new gene, form or morph is a data
  row; a new kingdom is a table plus a body.
- Open for that rework: how many stats, upkeep per kingdom, the food graph, the names.

## 7. What happens to today's build

- **Stays:** the layered sim, landmarks, variants and mutation, recipes and keys, groups, arrivals, the fit
  heatmap, the tree and spindle, the species card, the dev panel.
- **Changes:** a split becomes "the line comes home", or "take this rare variant on a run". Income comes from held
  slots instead of everything alive. Buying tree nodes turns into revealing recipes. Founding costs move to runs.
- **Waits:** genome v2, winning wild holders, lazy regions, a bigger home island, eras, importing genome codes,
  active abilities, the sea line.

## 10. Options we looked at

Five roles (lineage, gardener, breeder, shaper, naturalist) and three mixes: *the lineage on a changing island*,
*runs that fill the garden*, *the living loadout*. You picked the second, built cheaply, with slots the sim makes,
and took from the others: the lineage as the character, slots that boost each other and a moving world, breeding
kept scarce, the feeling of shaping land through what you place, readable animals and rare finds. Terra Nil stays a
main reference for the land coming alive.
