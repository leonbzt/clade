# First 30 minutes: strawman

**[Proposal]** written by AI on 25 Oct for you to cut. Nothing here is decided. Facts marked *(check)* go to the
researcher before they reach the game.

The setup is one pioneer plant and one slot. Relationship type 1 comes in session 1, and type 2 opens session 2
(systems S24). It assumes the first run is always a pioneer plant (Q5) and uses the founder colony (S4 C).

**Your answers (25 Oct, now in the brief):** runs start at an ancestor node · first release at ~12 min · the home is
fixed in run 1 · the seabirds move to session 2. Acts 6–7 and the session 2 section below are reworked to fit.
Later the same day: keep all 5 cut candidates · the cliffhanger stays · the end of the line is a real node, such as seed
plants. Acts 1–4 are now [slice 01](slices/01-found-to-release.md).

- **Goal chain, one at a time:** cover 10 tiles → reach Mosses → release → found your next species → reach
  Vascular plants.
- **Run 1 has no biomass.** The colony makes cover and genes. Favouring a variant costs genes. Release turns cover
  and fit into biomass per second, forever.
- **Not in the first 30 minutes:** guild resources, bundles, slot 2, seasons, tending moments.

## Real facts it leans on

- **Verified** ([research](research/progression-games.md)): a gull colony formed on Surtsey from the mid-1980s. It
  moved 45–50 kg N/ha/yr into the colony, against 1–2 kg elsewhere, and colony areas are far richer. That's why the
  gull edge is bigger than the moss edge.
- **Checked 25 Oct** in [research/moss-facts.md](research/moss-facts.md), with codex lines. Reviving after drying,
  the peristome, the Surtsey dates, sea rocket and the growth forms hold up. Hair points only "are thought to" cut
  water loss. **Decided:** the key innovation is the leafy shoot, and the peristome comes later for True mosses. The
  first home is a steam vent, where Surtsey's first mosses grew.
- **Still from memory, check before shipping:** wind carrying spores across oceans, trees evolving several times, the
  defining moss traits. The soil-building claim still needs a direct source.

## Critic's notes

1. **A deep-time tree on a young island. Decided: runs start at an ancestor node.** The island's order is a loose
   echo of plant history: plants that need no soil come first, and plants that need soil come later. The echo is loose,
   because Surtsey's first plant was a seed plant on the beach (sea rocket, *Cakile*, 1965). Mosses followed in 1967. The codex shouldn't claim
   more than that. One thing to settle when we curate nodes: "tree" is a growth form, not one clade. It evolved several
   times (scale trees, giant horsetail relatives, tree ferns, seed plants), so the end of the spine needs a real node, e.g. seed plants.
2. **The minigame can collapse into "set the split once".** Dry spells are the only thing that keeps it live. If the
   prototype shows that, minutes 1–11 are empty. Test it first, with fake numbers.
3. **Minute 15 is the riskiest dead spot:** an empty slot plus a wait. Keep it under 60 s, filled by the release
   aftermath.
4. **Concept load.** About 16 new ideas in 30 minutes, one every ~2 min. **Decided: keep all of them**, including
   the cheapest cut candidates (naming, the slot teaser, the incumbent line, standing rules, the beach fit).
5. **Decided: the gulls open session 2.** The codex still must not suggest the gulls came because of your plants.
   Minutes 20–27 are now quiet; the cliffhanger at 27:00 and the seabirds at 28:00 have to carry them.
6. **Dry spells aren't seasons** (seasons are cut from version 1). They're a minigame rule; keep them that way.
7. **A moss carpet has to read well at garden scale,** and an early land plant has to look appealing as a type
   specimen. Both are questions for the art spikes (Q7).
