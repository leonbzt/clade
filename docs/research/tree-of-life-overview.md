# Tree of life overview (planning map)

## Summary
- Plants, fungi and animals are three separate branches. Plants sit in Archaeplastida. Fungi and animals are cousins inside Opisthokonta (Holomycota vs Holozoa).
- Plants: green algae -> land plants (liverworts / mosses / hornworts) -> vascular (lycophytes) -> ferns -> seed plants (gymnosperms) -> flowering plants.
- Fungi: chytrids -> Mucoromycota (incl. mycorrhizal Glomeromycota) -> Dikarya (Ascomycota, Basidiomycota). Lichens are a fungus plus an alga, not a clade.
- Animals: sponges -> cnidarians -> bilaterians, split into protostomes (ecdysozoans, spiralians) and deuterostomes. Land was taken many separate times.
- Real island colonisers go: microbes and lichens / mosses / ferns, wind-blown insects, seabirds, then seabird guano drives plants. Mammals come last.
- Ages are rough. Molecular clocks and fossils disagree, sometimes by hundreds of millions of years. Treat every "Ma" below as a game-grade guess.
- Sourcing note: items marked **[search]** come from a search result (linked). Items marked **[background]** are standard textbook knowledge I did not re-verify in this pass. Wikipedia links point to the relevant pages for checking.

## 1. The big structure (eukaryotes)
- Opisthokonta is the group holding both animals and fungi. Origin about 700-1,000 Ma, with wide error bars. **[search]** https://biologydirect.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1745-6150-6-26
- Opisthokonta splits into two sister branches. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthokont
  - Holomycota: fungi, plus their single-celled relatives (Rozellida / Cryptomycota, nucleariid amoebae).
  - Holozoa: animals, plus choanoflagellates, filastereans, ichthyosporeans.
- Choanoflagellates are the closest living relatives of animals. Good "door" for a pre-animal stage. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choanoflagellate
- Archaeplastida: the lineage that got chloroplasts by swallowing a cyanobacterium. Three branches: glaucophytes, red algae, Viridiplantae (green algae + land plants). **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeplastida
- Green lineage: chlorophytes (most green algae) and streptophytes. Land plants come from within the streptophyte algae. The closest living algal relatives are the Zygnematophyceae (conjugating algae). **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streptophyta
- Siblings at the top: Archaeplastida and Opisthokonta are far apart. Plants are NOT closer to fungi than to animals in any useful sense. Fungi are closer to animals than to plants.
- Common mistake: fungi are not plants. They are a sister of animals' branch.

## 2. Plants (Embryophytes and before)
Dates are very uncertain. A recent molecular study puts land-plant origin at 980-682 Ma (Precambrian), while the older fossil-based view is about 515-470 Ma. **[search]** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8321542 (the search tool returned this link next to that claim; I did not open it, so check it before quoting). Use ~500 Ma for the game (first fossil spores).

| Clade | Key innovation (recipe gene idea) | Rough age | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Streptophyte / green algae | Multicellular filaments, desiccation tolerance, cell-wall pectin | > 500 Ma | Zygnematophyceae are the sister of land plants |
| Early land plant (embryophyte) | Cuticle (waxy skin), embryo kept on parent, sporopolenin spores, sporophyte | ~470-500 Ma | Matches the start of Clade |
| Liverworts | Flat thallus, oil bodies, simple rhizoids | ~450+ Ma | Sister of mosses (see below) |
| Mosses | Stomata-like pores on the sporophyte, leafy shoots, peat-forming (Sphagnum) | ~450+ Ma | Sister of liverworts |
| Hornworts | Pyrenoid (CO2 concentrating), horn-like sporophyte | ~450+ Ma | Third bryophyte lineage |
| Lycophytes (club mosses) | Vascular tissue (xylem), true roots / microphylls | ~420 Ma | Giant tree lycophytes made Carboniferous coal |
| Ferns (monilophytes) | Megaphylls (broad leaves), fiddlehead croziers | ~360-400 Ma | Sister to seed plants (together euphyllophytes) |
| Gymnosperms | Seed, pollen, wood (secondary growth) | ~320-350 Ma | Conifers, cycads, ginkgo, gnetophytes |
| Angiosperms | Flower, fruit, double fertilisation, vessels | Fossils ~130 Ma; one molecular estimate ~223 +/- 25 Ma | Dates disagree a lot |
| Monocots | One seed leaf, parallel veins, fibrous roots | ~130 Ma | Orchids, palms, grasses |
| Eudicots | Tricolpate pollen, most flowering plants | ~125 Ma | Roses, asters, oaks, legumes |
| Grasses (Poaceae) | Wind pollination, basal growth (survive grazing), C4 photosynthesis in some | Grass ~60-70 Ma; C4 spread ~30 Ma | Needs grazers and fire to flourish |

Sources and flags:
- Angiosperm crown ~223 Ma +/- 25, and five main mesangiosperm lineages split within about 9 Myr. **[search]** https://ntlib.org.cn/asset/detail/0/20628644178 (low-quality host; paper is a real timetree study, check on a journal site before quoting).
- Monocot / eudicot split estimates conflict (210 vs 340 Ma in two old studies). **[search]** https://courses.botany.wisc.edu/botany_940/05Frontiers/papers/1Palmer.pdf
- Other rows **[background]**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryophyte , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryophyte , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycopodiopsida , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnosperm , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae

Things to know:
- Bryophytes: the old view says "bryophytes are one clade". Recent work often supports bryophyte monophyly (liverworts + mosses + hornworts as one clade, sister to vascular plants), but it is still argued. Liverworts + mosses are the most solid sister pair. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryophyte
- Ferns and lycophytes are NOT one group. Lycophytes branched earlier. Ferns are closer to seed plants than to club mosses.
- "Gymnosperms" is a real clade (conifers, cycads, ginkgo, gnetophytes) but it is a sister of angiosperms, not an ancestor.
- Pioneer fact: lichens, mosses and ferns come first on bare rock because they have tiny spores and need no seed bank.

## 3. Fungi
Fungi diverge from other opisthokonts early. Fungal dating converged recently, but ranges are wide. **[search]** https://biologydirect.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1745-6150-6-26 ; also Lutzoni lab supplement https://lutzonilab.org/wp-content/uploads/Lutzoni-et-al-supplement.pdf

| Group | Key innovation | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rozellida / early fungi | Parasite of other microbes, spore | Parasites | Holomycota base |
| Chytrids (Chytridiomycota and kin) | Flagellated swimming spores, still aquatic | Decomposers, amphibian parasite (Bd) | Chitin wall already there |
| Mucoromycota | Fast hyphae, sugar-loving moulds | Decomposers, some root partners | Bread moulds |
| Glomeromycota (arbuscular mycorrhiza) | Arbuscules: tree-like structures inside root cells | Mycorrhiza (swap phosphorus for sugar) | ~400+ Ma, likely helped plants onto land |
| Ascomycota | Ascus (spore-sac), huge range of lifestyles, lichenisation | Decomposer, pathogen, yeast, lichen partner (most lichens) | Largest fungus phylum |
| Basidiomycota | Basidium (spore-bearing club), mushroom fruiting body, clamp connections | White-rot decomposer (lignin breakdown), ectomycorrhiza, rusts and smuts | Mushrooms, bracket fungi |

- Ascomycota + Basidiomycota form Dikarya (cells with two nuclei for a time).
- Lichen = fungus (usually ascomycete) + alga or cyanobacterium. Lichenisation evolved several times. It is a lifestyle, not a clade. Nice for the game: a "door" that unites two lineages. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen
- Mycorrhiza is ancient. Fossil Rhynie chert (~410 Ma) shows fungi inside early plants. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbuscular_mycorrhiza
- White-rot basidiomycetes that digest lignin: a popular idea links them to the end of Carboniferous coal formation. Still debated, say "may have". **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_rot
- Other: yeasts are fungi in several groups. Truffles are ascomycetes.

## 4. Animals, big structure
Bilaterian phyla diverge roughly 400-700 Ma by one study (wide). **[search]** https://biologydirect.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1745-6150-6-26 . The rest **[background]**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateria

- Sponges (Porifera): filter-feeding pump with collar cells, no true tissues. Sea only.
- Ctenophores (comb jellies): a hot debate about whether they or sponges are the sister of all other animals. Do not show this as settled.
- Cnidarians: stinging cells (nematocysts), radial body, nerve net. Corals, jellies. Sea (and fresh water hydra).
- Bilateria: left-right body, a head end, mesoderm, through-gut. Splits into:
  - Protostomia
    - Ecdysozoa (moulting): arthropods, nematodes, tardigrades, onychophorans. Innovation: cuticle that is moulted. 
    - Spiralia (incl. lophotrochozoans): molluscs, annelids, flatworms. Innovation: spiral cleavage, trochophore larva. Shells in molluscs.
  - Deuterostomia: echinoderms (five-fold symmetry, water vascular system), hemichordates, chordates (notochord, dorsal nerve cord, post-anal tail). Vertebrates add a skull and backbone; jaws; then limbs.
- Land invasions (separate events):
  - Arthropods: several times. Myriapods and arachnids (scorpions, mites), hexapods. ~430-480 Ma (Silurian-ish; range shaky). **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_animal
  - Snails and slugs: more than once (pulmonates, and operculate land snails).
  - Nematodes and tardigrades: tiny, in soil water films, probably early.
  - Annelids: earthworms (clitellates).
  - Vertebrates: tetrapods (~370 Ma, Devonian). Then amniotes (the shelled egg, ~315 Ma).
  - Flatworms (land planarians), velvet worms.
- Common mistake: insects are inside Crustacea (Pancrustacea). Hexapods came from a crustacean-like ancestor on land.

## 5. Focus groups

### 5a. Insects / arthropods
- Insect origin about 479 Ma (Early Ordovician), flight about 406 Ma (Early Devonian), main lineages by ~345 Ma. Holometabola diversified in the Early Cretaceous in that paper's dating. **[search]** https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/phylogenomics-resolves-the-timing-and-pattern-of-insect-evolution (Misof et al. 2014, Science). Note: that Holometabola date surprised me; many sources say Carboniferous origin. Disagreement; flag it.
- Hexapods: springtails (Collembola), proturans, diplurans are wingless "entognaths". Springtails: soil detritivores, tiny, among the first on new land. Rhynie chert ~410 Ma fossil. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springtail
- Bristletails and silverfish (Archaeognatha, Zygentoma): wingless true insects. Ancestral.
- Pterygota (winged): Wings (a single origin, flight). Odonata (dragonflies) and mayflies: cannot fold wings, aquatic larvae, predators.
- Neoptera: wing folding over the back, which allows hiding in crevices. Polyneoptera: cockroaches, termites (a kind of cockroach), mantids, grasshoppers, stick insects, earwigs.
- Hemiptera (true bugs): piercing-sucking mouthparts, plant sap. Aphids, cicadas.
- Holometabola (complete metamorphosis: larva, pupa, adult): the larva and adult can use different food. Four giant orders:
  - Coleoptera (beetles): hardened forewings (elytra). Everything.
  - Hymenoptera (wasps, bees, ants, sawflies): wasp waist, haplodiploidy, sociality, bees as pollinators.
  - Lepidoptera (moths, butterflies): scale wings, a long tongue, pollinators, herbivorous larvae. Tied to angiosperms.
  - Diptera (flies): one pair of wings, halteres. Pollinators, decomposers, parasites.
- Roles: detritivore (springtails, silverfish, beetles, fly larvae), herbivore (grasshoppers, caterpillars, aphids), pollinator (bees, butterflies, flies, beetles), predator (dragonflies, mantids, ground beetles, wasps), parasite (many wasps, fleas, lice).
- Other arthropods worth a node: spiders (silk, venom, ~380 Ma), centipedes and millipedes.
- Source for roles and orders is general **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holometabola

### 5b. Birds
- Birds are living theropod dinosaurs. Key innovations: feathers (earlier than flight), wishbone, hollow bones, then powered flight. Archaeopteryx ~150 Ma. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird
- Crown birds: three main groups, supported strongly. **[search]** https://prumlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/prum_et_al_2015.pdf (Prum et al. 2015, Nature), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4405904
  - Palaeognathae: ostriches, emus, kiwi, tinamous. Flightlessness evolved several times (the "ratites" are not one flightless clade).
  - Galloanserae: Galliformes (chickens, quail) and Anseriformes (ducks, geese). Ground / water birds.
  - Neoaves: nearly everything else (>90% of species). Fast radiation near the K-Pg boundary (66 Ma). Early relationships inside Neoaves are poorly resolved; the "Columbea" and "Passerea" groups were not recovered by Prum.
- Examples inside Neoaves: seabirds (penguins, albatross, gulls: salt glands, waterproof plumage), parrots (Psittaciformes: curved beak, climbing feet, intelligence) and songbirds (Passeriformes, ~6,000 species, syrinx and learned song). Parrots are the sister of passerines, with falcons close; that is a surprise (falcons are not close to hawks). **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoaves
- Crown-bird radiation: some molecular clocks say Late Cretaceous origin of Neoaves. Dating is disputed. **[search]** second-hand via Wikipedia summary.
- Role on islands: seabirds fertilise, carry seeds on feathers, in guts (fruit-eaters).

### 5c. Mammals
- Mammals are synapsids (not descended from dinosaurs). Key innovations: hair / fur, milk (mammary glands), three middle ear bones, warm blood, diverse teeth. Placenta later. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal
- Three living groups:
  - Monotremes (platypus, echidnas): lay eggs, milk with no nipples.
  - Marsupials: short pregnancy, pouch.
  - Placentals (Eutheria): long pregnancy with a placenta. 
- Four placental superorders: Afrotheria (elephants, hyraxes, manatees, tenrecs, aardvark), Xenarthra (sloths, anteaters, armadillos), Euarchontoglires (rodents, rabbits, primates, treeshrews), Laurasiatheria (bats, carnivores, hoofed animals, whales, shrews, hedgehogs). Afrotheria and Xenarthra are Gondwana-born (~103 Ma split); the other two are Laurasian and sister groups (Boreoeutheria). **[search]** https://TimeTree.org/public/data/pdf/Murphy2009Chap71.pdf
- Crown ages: Placentalia about 88-120 Ma, orders mostly split before 66 Ma, and big radiation after the dinosaur extinction. **[search]** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4920333/table/RSTB20150132TB2 . Methods disagree.
- Main orders **[background]**: Rodentia (gnawing incisors; ~40% of species), Chiroptera (bats: flight, echolocation, the only flying mammals; often first mammals on remote islands), Primates, Carnivora, Artiodactyla (even-toed, includes whales), Perissodactyla, Eulipotyphla.
- Roles: grazers (hoofed animals), seed dispersers (bats, rodents), predators (carnivorans), insectivores (shrews, bats).

### 5d. Snails (short)
- Gastropoda: a mollusc class, a single coiled shell, a muscular foot, rasping tongue (radula). Marine origin, many forms. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropoda
- Land snails and slugs: pulmonates evolved a lung out of the mantle cavity (also freshwater). Shell often lost (slugs, several times). Operculate land snails are a separate land invasion. **[background]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_snail
- Roles: herbivore / detritivore / fungus eaters, some carnivorous snails. Calcium (shell) limited.
- Island note: snails raft on birds or logs. Famous for islands radiations (Hawaii).

## 6. Island colonisation
Surtsey (Iceland, born 1963):
- First insect logged May 1964; first vascular plant, a sea rocket (Cakile), in 1965, on the shore (sea drift). **[search]** https://english.surtsey.is/adflutningur-lifvera/ , https://www.allaboutbirds.org/news/?p=52967 (sources disagree on the exact sea rocket species name and some dates).
- Birds arrived early; guillemots and fulmars nested ~1970. Insect-eating birds (snow bunting, wagtail, pipit) followed the insects.
- Plant growth stalled until a gull colony formed (1985-86). Birds then brought seeds and fertiliser. Bird dispersal became main route; wind-dispersed species followed after ~1990.
- Plant counts differ by source (69 species by 2013 vs 78 later).

Krakatau / Anak Krakatau (Indonesia):
- Colonisation pattern repeats itself early on: coastal plants and pioneers have good dispersal. Later arrivals in the interior (bird and bat-dispersed trees) are much less predictable. **[search]** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC279581 and https://www.pbs.org/wildindonesia/island/9.html (partial; I did not open them).
- Birds track vegetation: shorebirds and insectivores early, fruit-eaters when figs fruited (1985), predators later. Butterflies: 41 species recorded by 1990, 18 of them since 1986. **[search]** https://images.peabody.yale.edu/lepsoc/jls/1990s/1992/1992-46(2)83-Thornton.pdf
- After the 1883 eruption (**[background]**): cyanobacteria and ferns came first, then grasses and coastal trees. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakatoa

Rough order for the game (a synthesis, **proposal**-grade):
1. Microbes, cyanobacteria, lichens.
2. Wind: mosses, ferns (spores), springtails, flies, spiders (ballooning).
3. Sea drift: coastal seed plants (sea rocket, lyme grass), some beetles.
4. Seabirds, then their guano and seeds: grasses, bigger plants, gulls' colony changes soil.
5. Insects follow plants; insectivorous birds follow the insects.
6. Fruit-eating birds and bats, big plants.
7. Reptiles, snails, rafted animals, then land mammals (rarely without humans).
- Not reached naturally: most big mammals, amphibians (salt-intolerant), earthworms (sea barrier).

## 7. Game hooks

Good doors (one clear innovation to breed towards):
- Cuticle + embryo -> first land plant.
- Vascular tissue (xylem) -> club mosses.
- Megaphylls / leaves -> ferns.
- Seed + pollen -> gymnosperms (a big step: drought and cold survival).
- Flower + fruit -> angiosperms. Could split on pollinator (insect, wind, bird) and fruit type.
- C4 / basal growth -> grasses.
- Arbuscular mycorrhiza (fungus enters roots) -> opens plants and fungi together.
- Lichenisation (fungus + alga) -> lichen.
- Lignin-digesting enzymes -> white-rot fungi.
- Collar-cell colony -> sponge. Nematocyst -> cnidarian. Bilateral body + gut -> worms.
- Moulting cuticle -> arthropod. Wings -> insects. Wing folding -> neopterans. Metamorphosis -> beetle / bee / butterfly / fly.
- Pulmonate lung -> land snails. Vertebral limb -> tetrapod.
- Feathers then flight -> birds. Hair and milk -> mammals. Echolocation + flight -> bats.

Sister groups (can branch from one ancestor):
- Liverworts / mosses (with hornworts as third bryophyte branch).
- Lycophytes vs the "euphyllophytes" (ferns + seed plants).
- Ferns vs seed plants. Gymnosperms vs angiosperms.
- Monocots vs eudicots (with magnoliids and basal lineages outside).
- Ascomycota vs Basidiomycota (Dikarya). Chytrids vs everything else.
- Fungi vs animals (Holomycota vs Holozoa), both from the same ancestor.
- Sponges / ctenophores vs rest. Protostomes vs deuterostomes. Ecdysozoa vs Spiralia.
- Insects and crustaceans (Pancrustacea). Springtails vs true insects. Dragonflies vs neopterans. Holometabola groups sit together.
- Palaeognaths vs neognaths. Galloanserae vs Neoaves. Parrots vs songbirds.
- Monotremes vs therians (marsupials + placentals). Afrotheria vs Xenarthra, Euarchontoglires vs Laurasiatheria.
- Birds vs crocodiles (both archosaurs). Mammals are not close to birds.

Traps:
- Fungi are not plants. Termites are cockroaches. Ratites are not one clade. Insects are crustaceans. Lichen is not a clade. Falcons are near parrots. Whales are even-toed ungulates. Bryophytes are debated. Dates swing widely.
