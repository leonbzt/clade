# Fish and seabird kit material

*Research only, 17 Nov. No kits drafted.* Why: PR #3 found that open-water fish and birds have few named parts, so
their kits lean on traits. This page lists, per roster species, real parts by kit place (`front`, `eyes`, `limbs`,
`sides`, `back`, `tail`) and then trait material (behaviour, physiology, life history), with one card fact each.

**Caveat:** written from model knowledge in a 5-minute pass; the sources are the standard references to check each
fact against (FishBase, Animal Diversity Web, Birds of the World, Wikipedia), not pages fetched in this session.
Numbers marked ~ are rough. Verify before a card ships.

## The thin places, in one line
Fish: `limbs` = paired fins, `back` = dorsal fin(s), `sides` = lateral line and scales, `tail` = caudal fin and
peduncle. Birds: `limbs` = wings and feet together, `back` = plumage, `sides` = almost nothing. So for both, `sides`
and `back` are where traits must stand in for parts.

## Fish

### European anchovy (*Engraulis encrasicolus*), anchovies
- **front:** huge gape under a pig-like snout; ram filter feeding through long gill rakers.
- **eyes:** big eyes; polarisation-sensitive cone layout (anchovies are a classic case).
- **sides:** silver guanine mirror scales (counter-shading), lateral line used for schooling.
- **back / tail:** small single dorsal; forked caudal.
- **Traits:** schooling (millions; school as defence), filter vs particulate feeding switch, batch spawning
  (spawns many times a season, pelagic eggs), lives ~3–4 years, matures in ~1 year, diel vertical migration.
- **Card fact:** "Opens its mouth wide and swims: the gill rakers sieve plankton like a net."
- Sources: FishBase *Engraulis encrasicolus*; Novales Flamarique & Hawryshyn on anchovy polarisation vision.

### Clown anemonefish (*Amphiprion ocellaris*), damselfishes
- **front:** small mouth with single row of teeth; pops and clicks (sound from jaw snapping).
- **eyes:** UV-sensitive vision (sees UV patterns on other clownfish).
- **sides:** white bars; mucus coat that keeps the anemone from stinging (acclimation by touching).
- **Traits:** sequential hermaphrodite, male first (largest becomes female when she dies); size hierarchy in the
  anemone, subordinates stay small; male guards and fans eggs on rock near the anemone; ~10 day pelagic larva, then
  smells its way home.
- **Card fact:** "When the female dies, the biggest male turns into her."
- Sources: Animal Diversity Web *Amphiprion ocellaris*; Buston 2003 (Nature, size hierarchy).

### Stoplight parrotfish (*Sparisoma viride*) and bumphead parrotfish (*Bolbometopon muricatum*), parrotfishes
- **front:** fused teeth beak (the parrot beak); **pharyngeal mill** in the throat grinds coral rock to sand.
- **eyes:** colour vision; bumphead has a forehead hump above.
- **limbs:** swims mainly with the pectoral fins (labriform), tail only for bursts.
- **back:** bumphead's **head hump**, used for head-butting rivals (ramming recorded, Muñoz et al. 2012).
- **sides:** large scales; stoplight changes colour and sex (initial to terminal phase).
- **Traits:** some parrotfish sleep in a **mucus cocoon** (stoplight does at night); protogynous sex change;
  terminal males hold harems; bumpheads school and sleep in caves; a big bumphead makes ~5 t of sand a year (~,
  often quoted for the species); bumphead is slow-growing, ~40 years, overfished.
- **Card facts:** "Eats coral and poops white sand." / "Bumpheads ram each other head-first; you can hear it."
- Sources: Bellwood & Choat on parrotfish feeding; Muñoz et al. 2012 PLoS ONE (headbutting); Grutter et al. 2011
  (mucus cocoon).

### Giant moray (*Gymnothorax javanicus*), morays
- **front:** **pharyngeal jaws** that shoot forward to drag prey in (Mehta & Wainwright 2007, Nature).
- **eyes:** poor eyes; hunts by smell (big tube nostrils).
- **limbs:** none (no pectoral or pelvic fins).
- **sides / back:** scaleless thick skin with mucus; dorsal fin runs the whole back.
- **Traits:** gapes and pumps to breathe (looks threatening, isn't); hunts with groupers cooperatively, the
  grouper signals with a head shake (Bshary et al. 2006); can carry ciguatoxin; lives in holes, ambush.
- **Card fact:** "Has a second set of jaws in its throat that lunge out and pull prey down."
- Sources: Mehta & Wainwright 2007; Bshary et al. 2006 PLoS Biology.

### Yellowfin tuna (*Thunnus albacares*), tunas
- **front:** conical small teeth; ram ventilation (must swim to breathe).
- **eyes:** large eyes; warm eyes and brain? (that is bluefin and swordfish; yellowfin is only partly warm, keep out).
- **limbs:** pectorals fold into **grooves** for streamlining.
- **back:** long yellow second dorsal and anal fins (sickle-shaped in big adults); **finlets** along back and belly.
- **sides:** **red muscle** core with counter-current heat exchanger (rete mirabile) keeping the swimming muscle
  warmer than the sea; keels on the peduncle.
- **tail:** lunate (crescent) caudal, thunniform swimming, ~75 km/h bursts (~, often quoted).
- **Traits:** never stops swimming; schools with dolphins and under floating logs; spawns almost year round in warm
  water, millions of eggs; grows fast, matures ~2 years, lives ~7–9.
- **Card fact:** "Can't stop swimming, or it stops breathing."
- Sources: FishBase *Thunnus albacares*; Block & Stevens, *Tuna: Physiology, Ecology and Evolution* (2001).

### Blacktip reef shark (*Carcharhinus melanopterus*) and tiger shark (*Galeocerdo cuvier*), ground sharks
- **front:** rows of replacing teeth (tiger: serrated cockscomb teeth that saw turtle shell); ampullae of Lorenzini
  on the snout (sense electric fields).
- **eyes:** nictitating membrane; tapetum for low light.
- **limbs:** stiff pectoral fins (lift, not paddling); claspers on males.
- **sides:** dermal denticles (tooth-skin, drag-reducing); lateral line.
- **back:** black-tipped first dorsal (blacktip); tiger's stripes fade with age.
- **tail:** heterocercal (top lobe longer).
- **Traits:** blacktip: viviparous, 2–5 pups after ~10–16 months, pups use mangrove/reef nursery shallows; tiger:
  the only ground shark with **aplacental** live birth (ovoviviparous), litters up to ~80; tiger eats almost
  anything (turtles, seabird fledglings at Hawaii's French Frigate Shoals, licence plates); tonic immobility when
  flipped.
- **Card facts:** "Tiger sharks wait for albatross chicks' first flight." / "Shark skin is made of tiny teeth."
- Sources: Meyer et al. 2010 / Weng 2012 (tiger sharks and fledglings); Animal Diversity Web.

## Seabirds and shorebirds

### Herring gull (*Larus argentatus*), gulls
- **front:** hooked bill with a **red spot** (chicks peck it to make the parent regurgitate; Tinbergen's classic
  study).
- **eyes:** pale yellow iris, orange eye-ring.
- **limbs:** webbed feet (paddles; **foot-paddling** on grass to bring up worms); long wings for soaring.
- **back:** grey mantle; plumage takes ~4 years to reach adult (brown to grey).
- **sides:** almost nothing named; waterproofing from the preen gland.
- **Traits:** **salt glands** above the eyes, drinks seawater and sneezes brine; drops shells onto rock to crack
  them; generalist, kleptoparasite; colonial, lifelong pairs, 2–3 eggs, both parents incubate ~30 days;
  counter-current heat exchange in the legs (stands on ice); lives ~30 years.
- **Card fact:** "Chicks peck the red spot on the parent's bill to ask for food."
- Sources: Tinbergen & Perdeck 1950; Birds of the World *Larus argentatus*.

### Eurasian oystercatcher (*Haematopus ostralegus*), oystercatchers
- **front:** chisel bill; **two learned techniques**, hammerers and stabbers, passed parent to chick; bill tip
  grows ~0.4 mm a day (~) and wears to fit the technique.
- **eyes:** red eye and eye-ring.
- **limbs:** pink legs, no webbing.
- **Traits:** long-lived (~40 years recorded), late breeder (3–5 years), parents feed chicks (unusual among
  waders), loud piping displays.
- **Card fact:** "Learns from its parents to stab or to hammer; the bill wears to the style."
- Sources: Goss-Custard (ed.) *The Oystercatcher* (1996); Birds of the World.

## Gaps worth noting
- The roster has **one true seabird** (herring gull). If "seabird" kits matter, candidates on a tropical volcanic
  island: brown booby (*Sula leucogaster*: plunge diving, air sacs under the face skin), magnificent frigatebird
  (*Fregata magnificens*: can't land on water, red throat pouch, steals food, sleeps while flying), white tern
  (*Gygis alba*: lays its egg on a bare branch). Roster changes are the user's call; listed for `ideas.md` only.
- The roster mixes North Atlantic and Indo-Pacific species; the herring gull and oystercatcher are cold-water birds
  on a tropical island. Not a kit question, flagged only.
