# Kit material for the roster's other movers (research, 17 Nov)

Material for later kits, **not drafts**. One block per mover clade in `data/roster/clades.ts`, in the line order
`decisions.md` gives (periwinkles, urchins, shore crabs) and then the rest. Ragworms are the worm thread's page
(`worm-kit-names.md`); periwinkles have their own (`periwinkle-kit-sources.md`). Sessile life and producers are left
out (board pieces, direction §1 3a).

Each block: real parts by the kit's six places (front, head, limbs, sides, back, tail), two trait candidates, one
fact for a card. **Grade:** ✓ = read on the cited page today; ~ = known biology, check on the cited page before use.

## Summary
- Every mover clade maps onto the six places; the hard ones are radial bodies (urchins, sea stars), where "front"
  is the mouth underneath and "tail" has no real match. Suggest the kit lets a radial plan leave a place empty.
- The strongest kit material (many real, nameable parts and a clear ladder): shore crabs, octopuses, sea
  cucumbers, urchins, coconut crabs, marine iguanas. The thinnest: anchovies, tunas, gulls (few named parts beyond
  a generic fish or bird; their interest is behaviour).
- Several clades have one signature act that is a natural trait: the moray's second jaw, the sea cucumber's
  sticky tubules, the parrotfish's sleeping cocoon, the dog whelk's drill, the sea star's stomach outside its body.

## Urchins (line 3)
- Front: **Aristotle's lantern** (five teeth that grasp, scrape and tear) ✓. Head: none; diadematid urchins track a
  threat with their spines and the whole test may act as a **compound eye** ✓. Limbs: **tube feet** on the **water
  vascular system**, **madreporite** ✓. Sides: **spines** (movable, some venomous) ✓; **pedicellariae** (stalked jaws) ✓.
  Back: the **test** (fused calcite plates) ✓. Tail: no match (radial). Inner: **sphaeridia** (balance) ✓, five gonads ✓.
- Traits: venomous spines (*Diadema*-type) ✓; covering with shell and weed ~ (not on the page read).
- Larva: **echinopluteus** with 12 ciliated arms ✓.
- Fact: "Some red sea urchins are about 200 years old." ✓ [Wiki: Sea urchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_urchin)

## Shore crabs (line 4)
- Front: **chelae** (claws), in many crabs one **crusher** and one **cutter** ~; mouthparts (**maxillipeds**) ~.
  Head: **stalked eyes**, **antennae** ~. Limbs: four pairs of walking legs (**pereiopods**) ~; Sides: **carapace**
  with five teeth behind each eye ✓. Back: carapace; Tail: the tucked **abdomen**, which holds eggs in a female ~;
  **gonopods** in males ✓.
- Traits: **red phase** (crabs that delay moulting turn red, are stronger and more aggressive, but stand stress
  worse) ✓; **autotomy** (dropping a leg) ~.
- Brood: more than 400,000 eggs; larvae end as a **megalopa** ✓.
- Fact: "Reached North America in 1817 and spread 750 km up the Pacific coast in ten years." ✓ [Wiki: *Carcinus
  maenas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinus_maenas)

## Lugworms
- Front: **eversible proboscis** ~. Sides: **feathery gills** in pairs on the middle segments ✓. Back/Tail: the
  **tail shaft** and the coiled **cast** it leaves ✓. Inner: haemoglobin M101 carries up to 156 O₂ against our 4 ✓.
- Traits: a J- or U-shaped **burrow** ~; mass spawning ~.
- Fact: "One molecule of its haemoglobin carries up to 156 oxygen; ours carries 4." ✓ [Wiki: *Arenicola marina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arenicola_marina)

## Limpets
- Front: **radula** longer than the shell, 1,920 teeth in 160 rows of 12, made with **goethite** fibres, "the strongest
  natural material known" ✓. Head: tentacles, eyes ~. Limbs: the clamping **foot** ~. Back: conical **shell** up to 6 cm ✓.
- Traits: **home scar** (returns to its own spot, the shell ground to fit) ~; protandry (male first, then female) ~.
- Fact: "Its teeth are the strongest natural material known." ✓ [Wiki: Common limpet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_limpet)

## Sea cucumbers
- Front: 10–30 **oral tentacles**, shaped by diet (branched for filtering, digitate, shovel-like **peltate** for
  sand) ✓. Limbs: **tube feet** on the three lower rows (**trivium**) ✓. Sides: **catch collagen** body wall (soft
  enough to squeeze through gaps, then stiff) ✓. Tail: **respiratory trees** that breathe through the anus ✓.
- Traits: **Cuvierian tubules** (sticky threads shot at a predator, regrown in 1.5–5 weeks) ✓; **evisceration** ✓;
  **holothurin** (a soap-like toxin) ✓.
- Fact: "One animal swallows more than 45 kg of sand a year; a pearlfish may live in its rear end." ✓
  [Wiki: Sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber)

## Dog whelks
- Front: a **proboscis** with a **radula** that bores, helped by a shell-softening organ on the foot ✓ (the
  **accessory boring organ** ~). Back: a strong shell with a short **siphonal canal** ✓; **apertural teeth** grown
  against crabs ✓. Shape varies with wave exposure ✓.
- Traits: purple **dye** (used in Britain by the 7th century) ✓; egg capsules with nurse eggs ~.
- Fact: "Drills a hole in a mussel, then eats it over a day or a week." ✓ [Wiki: *Nucella lapillus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucella_lapillus)

## Sea stars
- Front: the **cardiac stomach**, pushed out through a 1 mm gap between a mussel's valves ✓. Limbs: rows of
  **tube feet** that pull the valves apart ✓. Head: **eyespots** at the arm tips ~. Sides: **pedicellariae** ~.
- Traits: arm **regeneration** ~; mass spawning (a moderate star makes about 2.5 million eggs) ✓.
- Fact: "It digests a mussel outside its own body." ✓ [Wiki: *Asterias rubens*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterias_rubens)

## Octopuses
- Front: **beak** and serrated **radula**; a **salivary papilla** with tiny teeth that drills shells ✓. Head: two
  camera **eyes** ✓. Limbs: eight **arms** with **suckers** that taste ✓ (two-thirds of 500 million neurons in the
  arms ✓). Sides: **chromatophores**, **iridophores**, **leucophores**, skin **papillae** ✓. Back: **ink sac** ✓. Tail:
  the **siphon** for jetting ✓. Inner: three hearts ✓.
- Traits: venom (all octopuses; lethal only in blue-ringed) ✓; **mimicry** (mimic octopus) ✓; arm autotomy ✓.
- Brood: one breeding then death; up to 500,000 eggs in *O. vulgaris*; the female guards them ✓.
- Fact: "Most of its brain is in its arms, and the arms can taste." ✓ [Wiki: Octopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus)

## Parrotfishes
- Front: teeth in a tight mosaic forming a **beak** ✓; **pharyngeal teeth** that grind coral and rock ✓. Sides:
  scales; Tail: fins ~.
- Traits: a night **mucus cocoon** that hides its scent (an alarm if a moray breaks it) ✓; **sex change**, female to
  male (initial and terminal phase) ✓.
- Fact: "One humphead parrotfish makes about 90 kg of sand a year." ✓ [Wiki: Parrotfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parrotfish)

## Moray eels
- Front: oral jaws and **pharyngeal jaws** thrown forward to grab prey, unique among animals ✓. Head: a strong
  **nose** (ambush by smell) ✓. Limbs: none (no pectoral or pelvic fins in most) ✓. Sides: scaleless skin with
  **mucus**, toxic in some ✓.
- Traits: **hunting with groupers**, which call it with head shakes ✓; ciguatoxin in big species ✓.
- Fact: "It has a second jaw in its throat that lunges out." ✓ [Wiki: Moray eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moray_eel)

## Damselfishes (clownfish)
- Material ~ (not read today): **mucus** that stops the host anemone stinging; all born male, the largest becomes
  female; males guard eggs; farming algae lawns (other damselfishes). Check: [Wiki: Amphiprioninae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphiprioninae).

## Anchovies, tunas, ground sharks (open water)
- Anchovies ~: **gill rakers**, schooling, a huge mouth gape for filtering. Tunas ~: **red muscle** kept warm
  (regional endothermy), fins that fold into grooves, **finlets**. Sharks ~: **ampullae of Lorenzini** (electric
  sense), **lateral line**, replaceable teeth, **dermal denticles**; tiger sharks eat almost anything. Not read today:
  [anchovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engraulidae), [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna),
  [tiger shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_shark). Thin on parts; their kits would lean on traits.

## Sea turtles
- Material ~: **beak** (green turtles' serrated, for grass), **front flippers** that fly underwater, **salt glands**
  by the eyes ("tears"), nesting on the beach they hatched from, temperature-set sex. Check: [Wiki: Green sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sea_turtle).

## Ghost crabs
- Head: **stalked compound eyes** that swivel for 360° sight ✓. Limbs: legs fast enough for over 1 m/s, "arguably
  the fastest terrestrial invertebrates" ✓. Inner: a **gastric mill** whose teeth growl ✓. Burrows up to 1.3 m ✓.
- Traits: nocturnal; sound (claw strikes, leg stridulation, stomach growl) ✓.
- Fact: "It growls with teeth in its stomach." ✓ [Wiki: *Ocypode quadrata*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocypode_quadrata)

## Sandhoppers
- Tail: the abdomen flicked out from under the body for a jump of several inches ✓. Head: navigates by sun, moon
  and the blue light of the sea ✓. Brood: 13–15 eggs carried by the female ✓; buried 10–30 cm by day ✓.
- Fact: "It finds the sea by the colour of the sky over it." ✓ [Wiki: *Talitrus saltator*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talitrus_saltator)

## Rock crabs (Sally Lightfoot)
- Material ~: flattened body for crevices, very fast sideways sprint, cleans ticks off marine iguanas, jumps between
  rocks. Check: [Wiki: *Grapsus grapsus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapsus_grapsus).

## Land hermit crabs (coconut crab)
- Front: claws that break a coconut's pore ✓. Head: short, blunt, insect-like **antennae**, smell food from far ✓.
  Limbs: climbs trees, 10 m ✓. Sides: **branchiostegal lungs** in place of gills; adults drown ✓. Back: juveniles
  carry snail shells, adults grow a hard abdomen ✓.
- Traits: larvae released at the shore at dusk, 3–4 weeks at sea ✓; a life past 60 years ✓.
- Fact: "Up to 4 kg, legs over 90 cm across, and it drowns in the sea it was born in." ✓ (that it is the largest land arthropod ~) [Wiki: Coconut crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_crab)

## Iguanas (marine iguana)
- Head: nasal **salt glands**, sneezed out ✓. Limbs: long **claws** for gripping rock in surf ✓; dense bones as
  ballast ✓. Back: a row of **spines** for stability ✓. Tail: a **flattened tail** for swimming ✓.
- Traits: **basking** (needs 35–39 °C, loses up to 10 °C on a dive) ✓; **shrinks** up to 20% in a famine and grows back ✓;
  dives to 30 m, up to an hour ✓.
- Fact: "In a lean year it shrinks its own body by a fifth." ✓ [Wiki: Marine iguana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_iguana)

## Oystercatchers and gulls (shore birds)
- Material ~: oystercatchers' **bill** used two ways (stabbers cut a mussel's muscle, hammerers smash it), and the
  style is learned from parents; gulls **drop shells** on rock, have **salt glands**, steal (kleptoparasitism).
  Check: [Wiki: Eurasian oystercatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_oystercatcher),
  [Wiki: Herring gull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_herring_gull).
