# Worm kit names: fact-check

Researcher pass, 17 Nov 2026

Evidence tags: **[V]** = page read this session. **[K]** = background knowledge, URL not opened (verify before it goes in a tooltip).
Wikipedia pages fetched were thin, so several claims stay [K]; they are marked.

## Summary
- 14 nodes: 7 accurate as concepts (tentacles/palps, proboscis, copper jaws, eyes, chaetae/parapodia, epitoky, venom), 5 need a fix, 2 weak/replace (hook, bulb), with deep/pouch/gut borderline.
- `sclerite` is the wrong word for living annelids: scale worms (Polynoidae) have **elytra**. "Sclerite" is mostly a fossil/other-phyla term [K].
- `symbiont`: Olavius is a gutless **clitellate oligochaete** (not an errant polychaete) [V]; Riftia is a **sedentary** vent tube worm [K]. Wrong for an errant. No well-sourced errant sulfur-symbiont found.
- `progenesis` is real in annelids (Dinophilus gyrociliatus, Dinophilidae) [V], but Dinophilidae sit in Sedentaria/Orbiniida [V], and it is a tiny interstitial worm, so it fits "a smaller, early-breeding worm", not an errant of the main body plan.
- `bulb` has no real counterpart I could source: burrowing is by everted proboscis and peristalsis, anchored by body dilations [K]. `pouch`: polychaetes do brood, but via egg sacs, tubes or under the elytra, not a "pouch" [K].

## 1. front / `tentacles`: Tentacles, Feeding palps, Ciliated crown
- Tentacles: **Imprecise.** Polychaete heads carry **palps** (feeding/sensory) plus antennae [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete]. Rung 1 "Tentacles" is generic but fine.
- Feeding palps: **Accurate.** Errants use palps to sense and manipulate food.
- Ciliated crown: **Wrong for errants.** A "crown" (radiolar crown, branchial crown) is the filter-feeding fan of sedentary fanworms/featherdusters (Sabellidae) [K]; the Polychaete page contrasts sedentary tentacles for filtering with errant jaws and sense organs [V]. Better: **Nuchal organs** (paired ciliated chemosensory pits behind the head, typical of polychaetes) [K], or "Sensory antennae".
- Fact: "Bristle worms taste the water with ciliated pits called nuchal organs." [K, verify]
- Fit: palps yes; crown no (fanworm).

## 2. front / `proboscis`: Proboscis, Jawed proboscis, Copper jaws
- Proboscis: **Accurate.** Most polychaetes have jaws and an eversible pharynx used to grab food [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete]. Say "pharynx" or "everted pharynx" in tooltips; "proboscis" is the common term.
- Jawed proboscis: **Accurate.** Jaws are sclerotised collagen [V: same].
- Copper jaws: **Accurate for Glycera.** Four hollow jaws on a large proboscis, with atacamite (copper-based) biomineral [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycera_(annelid)]. Synchrotron work shows copper also as Cu(I) and in the unmineralised jaw base [V: https://www.pnnl.gov/publications/distribution-and-role-trace-transition-metals-glycera-worm-jaws-studied-synchrotron]. Caveat: Wikipedia says copper "may" catalyse the venom, not proven.
- Fact: "Bloodworm jaws are laced with a copper mineral, atacamite, and sit on hollow fangs wired to venom glands." [V]
- Fit: errant (Glycera is an errant burrower).

## 3. front / `hook` (rare): Hooked proboscis
- **Weak / not sourced.** I found no named polychaete structure "hooked proboscis". Hooks (uncini) in polychaetes are chaetae of sedentary worms [K]. Real alternatives in the same slot: **Pincer jaws** (Nereis/Platynereis pair of curved toothed jaws) [K], or **Maxillary apparatus** (Eunice and relatives, a multi-piece jaw set; Bobbit worm *Eunice aphroditois* is an ambush predator that lunges from a burrow) [K].
- Suggested name: "Maxillary jaws" or "Ambush jaws".
- Fact: "The Bobbit worm waits buried, antennae out, then snaps shut on passing fish." [K, verify]
- Fit: errant yes (Eunicida).

## 4. head / `eye`: Eyespots, Cup eyes, Lensed eyes
- Species have zero to four pairs of eyes; most are simple light/dark detectors, but Alciopidae have complex eyes that rival cephalopods and vertebrates [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete]. Alciopids are called "camera eyed worms", with a study titled "High-resolution vision in pelagic polychaetes" [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alciopidae]. Nereis has two pairs of eyes on the dorsal head [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nereis].
- Eyespots / Cup eyes: **Accurate** (pigment-cup ocelli are the standard simple eye) [K].
- Lensed eyes: **Accurate but exceptional**; pelagic alciopids, not typical nereids. Nereis is the cup-eye example, not the lens one. Fix the "cf. Nereis" note.
- Fact: "Some open-ocean bristle worms see with camera eyes that rival those of squid." [V]
- Fit: errant yes (Alciopidae are Errantia) [V].

## 5. sides / `chaetae`: Bristles, Paddle feet, Swimming paddles
- Parapodia are fleshy, paddle-like, highly vascularised, aid movement, often main respiratory surface; chaetae project from them [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete]. Nereis parapodia serve locomotion and gas exchange [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nereis].
- Bristles: **Accurate** (chaetae; "bristle worms").
- Paddle feet: **Accurate** (parapodia).
- Swimming paddles: **Imprecise as a rung**; same organ, so it is a specialisation. Real version: epitokes grow broadened, oar-like parapodia for swimming (heteronereis) [K, consistent with epitoky page V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitoky, which says epitokes are built for swimming]. Better rung: "Oar paddles" or "Swimming parapodia".
- Fact: "A bristle worm's paddle feet are also its gills." [V]
- Fit: errant core trait.

## 6. sides / `sclerite`: Sclerites, Scale rows, Overlapping scales
- **Wrong word.** Polynoidae have "scale-like elytra on the dorsal surface", usually covering most of the body; page never uses "sclerite" [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynoidae]. "Sclerite" in annelid context mostly means plates of fossil Cambrian "armoured" worms, or chaetal/jaw elements [K].
- Better names: **Elytra** (rung 1), "Scale rows" (OK as description), "Elytral cover" / "Scale armour".
- Elytra can be shed and regenerated; some are faintly bioluminescent and can leave glowing traces on a predator's mouth, making it a target [V: same].
- Fact: "Scale worms throw off a glowing scale to distract the predator that grabbed them." [V, "throw off" shedding and glow both on page; the decoy framing is [K], tone down if needed]
- Brood bonus: polynoids (e.g. Harmothoe) often brood eggs under the elytra [K, verify], which links nodes 6 and 11.
- Fit: errant (Polynoidae in Errantia [V]).
- Alternative same slot (defence): **fireworm chaetae**: Amphinomidae have "toxin-coated chaetae" that hurt on touch; complanine from *Eurythoe complanata* causes inflammation and is presumed to deter predators [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphinomidae]. Good defensive-sides rung ("Stinging chaetae").

## 7. back / `gut`: Gut gland, Long gut, Gizzard
- Gut gland: **Imprecise**; annelid digestive glands/caeca exist but the term is vague [K]. Better: "Digestive caeca".
- Long gut: **Accurate-ish** (diet-linked gut length) but generic.
- Gizzard: **Accurate** for earthworms (clitellates); in polychaetes a muscular pharynx/gizzard-like region exists in some (e.g. some syllids have a muscular proventriculus) [K]. Syllid "proventricle" is a better real term.
- Fact: "Syllid worms have a muscular, barrel-shaped proventricle that works like a gizzard." [K, verify]
- Fit: syllids are errant; earthworm gizzard is not.

## 8. back / `symbiont` (rare): Sulfur symbionts
- **Wrong clade for the cf. examples.** *Olavius algarvensis* is a gutless oligochaete (Clitellata, Tubificida), 12-25 mm, no mouth, gut or nephridia, depends on sulfide-oxidising and sulfate-reducing bacteria under its cuticle, in Mediterranean coastal sediment [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olavius_algarvensis]. Not a polychaete. *Riftia* (giant tube worm) is a sedentary vent siboglinid [K]; the Polychaete page notes giant tube worms live near hydrothermal vents [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete] (Riftia itself not mentioned).
- Replace/reframe: for an errant near a volcanic island, the vent-adjacent option is a **commensal/bacterial mat** idea, but I found no sourced errant with chemosymbiosis. Alternative same slot (back): **Bioluminescence** (scale worm glow, V above) or **Bone-eater root tissue** (Osedax, bone-eating polychaete with symbiotic bacteria, sedentary) [V partial: Osedax exists on Polychaete page].
- Keep only if the game accepts "borrowed from a relative". Name could be "Sulfur-eating bacteria".
- Fact: "Olavius has no mouth and no gut: its bacteria feed it." [V] (not an errant)

## 9. back / `progenesis` (rare): Progenesis
- **Real, but not an errant polychaete.** *Dinophilus gyrociliatus*: authors conclude the adult female's arrested development suggests progenetic origin; females 1.0-1.5 mm vs 2.3-3.1 mm in *D. taeniatus*; dwarf males ~50 micrometres, mature before hatching, fertilise females in the cocoon, die within 1-2 days [V: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5101659]. Phylogenetic position unresolved [V: same]; Dinophilidae listed under Orbiniida, Sedentaria [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinophilidae].
- Name: "Progenesis" is the correct term (precocious maturation of a larval-like form). Tooltip-friendly: "Dwarf breeder" / "Larval breeding".
- Fact: "Dinophilus males are 50 micrometres long and live for a day or two." [V]
- Fit: not errant; a miniaturised lineage.

## 10. tail / `bulb`: Burrowing bulb, Muscular bulb, Anchor bulb
- **Not a real posterior structure that I could source.** Polychaetes burrow by everting the pharynx/proboscis and peristalsis, anchoring with body swellings (terminal/dilation anchors) [K]; the Polychaete page says most species burrow or build tubes [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete]. Glycera's proboscis is large [V: Glycera page] and is used as a digging tool [K].
- Better names: **Hydrostatic anchor** (the rung "Anchor bulb" is the nearest real idea), "Proboscis burrowing", "Muscular body wall". For a tail: "Pygidial cirri" (real tail appendages) [K].
- Fact: "Bristle worms dig by blowing out their throat as a ram and anchoring their body with a swollen section." [K, verify with Trueman burrowing literature]
- Fit: errant burrowers (Glycera, Nephtys) yes.

## 11. tail / `pouch`: Brood pouch, Brood chambers, Brooding tube
- Polychaetes are mostly external spawners with trochophore larvae; "brooding" not described on the main page [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychaete]. Brooding exists: Autolytus syllids carry egg sacs on stolons [K, Wikipedia page failed to load]; some nereids brood in tubes (*Platynereis massiliensis*) [K]; polynoids brood under elytra [K].
- Fix: "Egg sac" (Autolytus) fits an errant best; "Brooding tube" fits tube-dwelling forms. "Brood pouch" itself: unsourced for polychaetes.
- Fact: "Autolytus females carry their eggs in a sac on their rear half." [K, verify]
- Fit: yes, mixed.

## 12. tail / `deep` (rare): Deep burrow
- **Game-invented.** Deep burrows exist (burrowing polychaetes, e.g. Arenicola's U-shaped burrow [K]) but there is no single "deep" anatomical trait. Better as a habit rather than a part.
- Alternative: **Interstitial life** (small worms among sand grains) or **Tube building**.

## 13. trait `venom`: Jaw venom, Paralysing venom, Tissue-dissolving venom
- Jaw venom: **Accurate** for Glycera: hollow jaws connected to venom glands that kill prey [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycera_(annelid)].
- Paralysing venom: **Plausible** [K] (Glycera toxins act on neurons); not read this session.
- Tissue-dissolving venom: **Unsupported.** I found no source; do not use. Better: "Neurotoxin" or "Stinging chaetae" (fireworms, toxin-coated chaetae [V: Amphinomidae page]).
- Eunice: venom claim unverified [K].
- Fact: "Glycera bites with four hollow fangs fed by venom glands." [V]
- Fit: Glycera and Amphinomidae are errant (Amphinomidae page does not say so itself [V]).

## 14. trait `epitoky`: Yolky eggs, Spawning form, Swarm spawning
- Epitoky: a sexually immature worm (atoke) changes into a sexually mature epitoke, in many species the reproductive part breaks off as a free-swimming stolon; built for swimming and reproduction, not feeding; die after release [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitoky].
- Yolky eggs: **Imprecise**; yolk is general oogenesis, not epitoky. Better: "Egg-filled epitoke" (Platynereis females look yellow with eggs [V]).
- Spawning form: **Accurate** ("epitoke").
- Swarm spawning: **Accurate**; epitokes rise to the surface on moon-timed schedules; some may use bioluminescence to keep swarms together [V: Epitoky page].
- Palolo worm (*Palola viridis*): rear body segments drop off ("schizogamy" per page; strictly the term for epitoky-linked budding is debated [K]) and float up to spawn on waning-moon nights in Oct-Nov (southern hemisphere); eaten in Samoa and Fiji (balolo) [V: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palolo_worm].
- Fact: "Every year the palolo worm's tail breaks off and swims to the surface to spawn, timed to the moon." [V]
- Fit: strongly errant (Nereididae, Eunicidae).
