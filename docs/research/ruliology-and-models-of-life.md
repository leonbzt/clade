# Ruliology, ruliobiology and other mathematical models of life

Researched 6 Nov 2026. Question: what is "ruliobiology", and which models of life, bodies and evolution (and which computational-biology approaches) could serve Clade? Marks: [src] = page read this session; [search] = only a search-result summary seen; [mem] = my background knowledge, link given but page not opened. Game-design findings already in `diverse-evolution-games.md`, `garden-models.md` and `real-evolution.md` are not repeated.

## Summary
- The term is real: **ruliobiology** (MathWorld entry; Wolfram essay of 23 Oct 2026). It sits on **ruliology** (Wolfram's name for the study of what simple rules do) and the ruliad. Not a field outside Wolfram's circle.
- The model: a cellular-automaton rule is the genotype, the pattern grown from one cell is the phenotype. Random point mutations are kept if fitness (mostly "lifetime before dying out") does not drop.
- Demonstrated, inside the toy: long plateaus and sudden jumps, lots of neutral mutations, many genotypes per phenotype, some targets unreachable. Speculative: that this explains why biological evolution works, "polyplexy", astrobiology.
- Reception: I found no evolutionary-biology review or rebuttal. Only a Hacker News thread ("old genetic programming concepts") and one small follow-up (Houghton, arXiv 2025). Treat as an unrefereed-by-biologists idea.
- The most Clade-usable parts are not the CA itself (too costly, wrong shape) but its *story*: neutral drift, then breakthrough; and old cheap models: niche model food webs, Raup/Thompson-style morphospace, adaptive-dynamics branching, Turing/CPPN coat patterns.
- Avoid for Clade: whole-cell models, assembly theory, AlChemy, Kauffman RAF sets, NCA training, anything needing GPU or learned nets at runtime.
- Ranked shortlist is at the end. All are *ideas*, not plans.

## 1. Wolfram's biology work

**The terms**
- *Ruliology*: Wolfram's name for the systematic study of simple rules and what they do. [search: https://wolframinstitute.org/research/ruliology , page not opened]
- *Ruliobiology*: "a proposed framework for studying the generation of biological-like forms" by simple rules under adaptive evolution; a pattern so generated is a "rulioorganism". [src: https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Ruliobiology.html] MathWorld cites the essay "'Endless Forms Most Beautiful and Most Wonderful': Ruliobiology and the Theoretical Foundations of Biological Form", 23 Oct 2026. [src: https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2026/10/endless-forms-most-beautiful-and-most-wonderful-ruliobiology-and-the-theoretical-foundations-of-biological-form/]
- The ruliad (the "entangled limit of all possible computations") is background, not needed for the biology. [mem]
- Not to be confused with Carroll's 2005 evo-devo book *Endless Forms Most Beautiful*; the title is Darwin's phrase.

**Paper 1: "Why Does Biological Evolution Work? A Minimal Model..." (May 2024; Complex Systems 33(4), 2025, pp. 387-488)** [src: https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/05/why-does-biological-evolution-work-a-minimal-model-for-biological-evolution-and-other-adaptive-processes/ ; journal: https://www.complex-systems.com/abstracts/v33_i04_a01/ [search]]
- Rule: 3-colour nearest-neighbour CA (k=3, r=1). 26 relevant cases, each can change to 2 other outcomes: 52 point mutations. Start from the "null" rule (pattern dies at once). Accept a mutation if the pattern's lifetime stays finite and does not get shorter. Infinite growth ("tumor") is rejected.
- Found: runs reach lifetimes such as 107, 162, 723 from single-cell seeds; in 100 runs the best kept rising, some with long plateaus. Progress comes in plateaus and sudden jumps, often from one mutation, with many rejected mutations in between. Neutral mutations let the rule drift until a combination pays off.
- Many genotypes per phenotype: one final rule used only 18 of 26 cases, so 3^8 = 6,561 genotypes give the same pattern.
- Exhaustive search, k=2, r=2, symmetric: 524,288 rules, 77,624 finite, only 77 distinct patterns, max lifetime 65, lifetime counts roughly power law (exponent about -3). Directed sampling of k=3, r=1 found a lifetime of 10,863 (a lower bound). Some patterns run 7,129 steps before dying; termination is undecidable in general (busy-beaver style).
- Multiway graph of all mutation paths: k=2, r=1 has 128 rules and only 15 reachable from null by adaptive evolution. Some high-lifetime rules are unreachable from null. A random path took 169 mutations where the shortest found in the graph took 9.
- Variants: crossover with populations of 4-50 rules "not obviously different" from mutation alone; allowing temporary fitness loss changed little.
- Wolfram's own analogies (his, not tested against data): punctuated equilibrium, branching like a tree of life, convergence, gene-regulation-like partial rule use, neutral drift.
- Speculative per the essay: that complexity comes mainly from computational irreducibility, that evolved rules are class-4-like, that evolution is "formally derivable", that fitness has no upper limit. Real-world counts (10^40 organisms) are rhetoric.

**Paper 2: "Foundations of Biological Evolution: More Results & More Surprises" (5 Dec 2024)** [search: https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2024/12/foundations-of-biological-evolution-more-results-more-surprises/]
- Width-based fitness instead of lifetime gives fairly similar results; stress on fitness-neutral evolution; a combined multiway graph for several fitness functions. Page not read in full.

**Paper 3: "What's Special About Life? Bulk Orchestration and the Rulial Ensemble..." (Nov 2025)** [src: https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/?p=71196]
- 4-colour 1D CAs, 50 steps, width 101, single seed. "Mutational complexity" = number of mutations needed to reach a target; simpler targets are reached faster. Evolved rules are more compressible than random rules; requiring robustness gives simpler, more "mechanoidal" solutions. Only 18 of 128 length-7 target sequences were reachable in the elementary-CA case. One exact solution took about 4,000 steps.
- The "rulial ensemble" (average over rules selected for a purpose, like a statistical ensemble) is aspirational; "no biological data is analysed"; claim that real fitness functions are computationally simple is asserted, not measured.

**Paper 4: ruliobiology essay (23 Oct 2026)** [src: the URL above]
- Model: symmetric 5-colour CA (white = void, plus purple, red, green, yellow), 125 rule cases, grown from one cell. Mostly lifetime fitness; also width, aspect ratio, colour fractions.
- Findings: breakthroughs separated by many neutral or harmful mutations. One 1,000-step run reached lifetime 580, another 1,445. Sample of 100,000 evolved forms: exotic shapes at a fraction of a percent; extremes in width often share strategies (a "pincer" form); selecting for width directly gives only modestly wider forms than width as a side effect of lifetime. Outline fractal dimension roughly Gaussian, mean about 1.3. Early development is conserved: with the first 10 steps frozen after a while (58 of 125 cases used), 30 steps not.
- "Polyplexy": a hierarchy of novelty, different things at each scale (unlike self-similarity). Name coined by him; no outside use found.
- Claims: computational irreducibility is the "raw material" selection works on; parts behave differently alone than in the whole ("bulk orchestration"); modular organ-like structures with random-looking "connective tissue". Convergence is only "approximate". The astrobiology section is explicitly speculative. Fitness functions are artificial; real fitness is not tested.

**Reception (thin)**
- I found no review, rebuttal or commentary by evolutionary biologists in indexed sources. Absence of search hits is not proof there is none.
- Hacker News thread on paper 1: little substance; one reader's summary "CA combined with pretty old genetic programming concepts"; some said the explanation was unclear. [src: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40245769]
- One manuscript excerpt on a ResearchGate page argues circularity: self-reproduction is built into the primitives and the programmer picks the fitness function that drives selection. Author and status unclear. [search: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/388377589_Why_Does_Biological_Evolution_Work_A_Minimal_Model_for_Biological_Evolution_and_Other_Adaptive_Processes]
- Follow-up use: Houghton (arXiv 2504.05096) adds group scoring ("max fitness in group", mimicking cooperation) and finds longer-lived rules evolve more easily. [search: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05096]
- Older Wolfram biology claims (NKS, 2002: selection's power "remarkably limited") were disputed by reviewers. [search: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/86037] The new work does not repeat that claim in the pages I read.
- My view, flagged as judgement: the plateau/neutral-drift/unreachable-region findings restate known results from neutral-network and fitness-landscape work (section 2) and from evolving-CA work (Mitchell, Crutchfield, Hraber 1994 [mem, not verified]). What is new is the exhaustive multiway enumeration for tiny rule spaces. The step from there to "why biology works" is untested.

**Cost and fit for Clade**
- One lifetime test = run the CA up to its lifetime on a growing row. At lifetime ~1,400 that is about 10^6 cell updates; 1,000 mutation tests per organism is roughly 10^9 updates (my estimate, not measured). Fine offline, wrong for runtime and for a hours-in-a-second budget.
- **Blunt:** a rulioorganism is a pattern, not a body with limbs and mouths; nothing maps to the six slots. As art, a baked gallery of 5-colour forms could look like fossils or microbial "ghosts". As mechanics, the useful part is the *pacing pattern* (hidden neutral drift, then a breakthrough, with some forms reachable only through stepping stones). That needs no CA.

## 2. Models of evolution, fitness and ecology

- **NK landscapes** (Kauffman). N loci; fitness = mean of N contributions, each depending on its own locus and K others, drawn at random. K tunes ruggedness (K=0: one easy peak; larger K: many local optima). Finding optima is NP-complete; for K>1 even local optima are PLS-complete. Bare model has no neutral plateaus (variants NKP/NKQ add them). Cost: a 2^(K+1) table per locus, trivial. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NK_model] Clade: random landscapes are illegible to a player; authored synergies read better (see `diverse-evolution-games.md`). Possible only as a hidden "valley to cross" for pacing.
- **Neutral networks / genotype-phenotype maps** (Schuster, Fontana, Wagner; RNA folding). Many genotypes fold to one structure; neutral sets are connected by point mutations; bigger neutral sets tend to bring more robustness and more reachable variants. Later work: epistasis fragments neutral networks, making outcomes history-dependent. [search: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4777517 ; https://www.tbi.univie.ac.at/newpapers/pdfs/TBI-p-2009-1.pdf] This is the real precedent for Wolfram's "neutral drift". Clade: the idea that hidden variation accumulates invisibly is cheap and good; the full map is not needed.
- **Quasispecies** (Eigen). n'_i = sum_j w_ij n_j with w_ij = A_j q_ij - D_i d_ij; above an error threshold the population disperses over all sequences. Selection acts on a mutant cloud, so a well-connected genotype can beat the fastest one. Seen in RNA viruses. Qualitative only; parameters can't be measured. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasispecies_model] Clade: interesting only. (Possible flavour: "robust lineage beats fragile fast one".)
- **Price equation.** Change in mean trait = Cov(fitness, trait)/mean fitness + a within-group term. Algebraic identity, unifies the breeder's equation, Fisher's theorem and replicator dynamics; not predictive alone (needs higher moments). [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_equation] Clade: a tidy way to *report* "why did the average trait shift" in a log (selection part vs mutation part). Interesting; low priority.
- **Replicator dynamics / evolutionary game theory.** x_i' = x_i (f_i - mean f). Equivalent to Lotka-Volterra; with fixed fitness it is winner-take-all; frequency dependence gives coexistence (Hawk-Dove), RPS cycles. Cost trivial. Already analysed in `garden-models.md` (stabilisers, kill-the-winner). [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replicator_equation via that file]
- **Moran and Wright-Fisher.** Finite-population drift: a neutral allele fixes with probability 1/N; W-F resamples N each generation, Moran replaces one at a time. [mem: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moran_process , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright%E2%80%93Fisher_model] Clade: drift matters only for small populations (a new lineage of a few eggs). One line of rule: "small colonies lose a trait by chance". Cheap (binomial draw).
- **Adaptive dynamics** (Metz, Geritz, Dieckmann). Mutants are rare and small; invasion fitness = growth rate of a rare mutant in the resident's equilibrium. The sign of its slope (selection gradient) gives the direction of change; at a convergence-stable fitness *minimum* the population can split into two coexisting morphs (evolutionary branching). Also shows "evolutionary suicide". Assumptions: rare mutation, small steps, resident at equilibrium. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_dynamics] Cost: a 1-D trait axis with competition kernel is a few lines. Clade: the best-principled answer to "when does one lineage become two species in a niche" (a crowded middle of a wide resource axis).
- **Bak-Sneppen** (1993). Ring of N species with random fitness; each step replace the least fit and its two neighbours with fresh random values. Self-organises to a threshold; avalanches of replacement give punctuated equilibrium and extinction-size distributions. Deliberately minimal, "not an accurate model of evolutionary biology"; empirical fit to fossils not shown on the page. Cost O(N) per step. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bak%E2%80%93Sneppen_model] Clade: a cheap generator of crash cascades along neighbouring niches; fun but arbitrary. Honourable mention.
- **Lotka-Volterra, paradox of enrichment, competition models.** Covered in `garden-models.md` and `layer-sim-ecology.md`.
- **Niche model of food webs** (Williams & Martinez 2000, Nature 404:180). Each of S species gets a niche value n in [0,1]; it eats all species in one contiguous interval of the niche axis whose size is set by the target connectance. Two inputs (S, connectance); matched 7 of the most complex empirical webs on 12 properties, about ten times closer than earlier two-input models; the author cautions it is not a law. [search: https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/simple-rules-yield-complex-food-webs/ ; https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2000/03/000309074625.htm] Cost O(S^2) once. Clade: a legible, cheap way to decide who eats whom among tiers; "prey interval on one axis" is also easy to draw.
- **Red Queen / Van Valen's law.** Extinction risk of a taxon is roughly constant with age (survival curves straight on a log plot), differs by ecological group. The arms-race explanation is contested ("court jester" says abiotic change dominates). [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Queen_hypothesis] Clade: constant hazard is a free, cheap extinction rule (no "old = safe"); the "treadmill" can be an `arms`-type rubber band (already in the toy).
- **Evolvability.** Capacity to generate heritable *adaptive* variation. Mechanisms: modularity (pleiotropy confined to modules), robustness plus cryptic variation, capacitors. Disputed: whether evolvability itself is selected; "heritability is not evolvability". [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolvability] Clade: the six-slot body is already modular; "a part change affects one slot only" is the evolvability argument, so keep slot effects independent unless a synergy is authored.

## 3. Models of bodies and development

- **Turing reaction-diffusion; Gierer-Meinhardt.** Two morphogens, a short-range activator and a long-range inhibitor, spontaneously make spots/stripes: du/dt = f(u,v) + Du lap(u), dv/dt = g(u,v) + Dv lap(v), with Dv >> Du. Evidence: marine angelfish pattern changes predicted by Turing's model (Kondo, 1995); zebrafish stripe re-spacing after ablation reproduced by simulation. Caveats from the same group's 2021 review and a Nat. Commun. paper: zebrafish mechanism is not classic reaction-diffusion (cell-cell interactions). [search: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8580470 ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/ncomms7971] Cost: a 32x32 grid, a few hundred steps, bakeable once per species seed. Clade: you know this; as a *coat/marking generator with 2-3 heritable parameters* it is cheap and honest. [Sheth et al. 2012 on Hox and digit Turing patterns: not verified.]
- **French flag / morphogen gradients** (Wolpert 1969). A gradient plus thresholds gives positional identity. [mem: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_flag_model] Clade: not needed beyond what the six places already do.
- **Raup's shell morphospace** (1966). Shell = logarithmic-spiral cone from W (whorl expansion), D (umbilicus), T (translation) [plus S, whorl shape]. Real shells fill only a small corner; are empty regions impossible or just unvisited? [search: https://sfipress.org/27-raup-1966 ; https://palaeo-electronica.org/content/pdfs/510.pdf] Cost: closed form per point. Clade: a species' body as a few continuous parameters, with a visible map of "forms seen / unseen" is an on-theme codex screen.
- **D'Arcy Thompson transformations** (1917). Related forms differ by a smooth coordinate warp of one outline. [mem: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Growth_and_Form] Cost: a shear/scale of part positions. Clade: 2-3 "shape genes" that warp the skeleton (long, deep, slender) give lots of look variety without new art; risk (Spore, Species): variety with no gameplay effect feels samey (`diverse-evolution-games.md`), so each warp should change a stat.
- **Hox-like identity and repeated modules.** Segment identity from a few master genes; repeated segments vary together; mutation of a "count" gene adds segments. [mem: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hox_gene] Clade: your slot system is this. A cheap addition is a repeat-count gene for Limbs/Sides.
- **Allometry and scaling (Kleiber; West-Brown-Enquist).** Metabolic rate B ~ M^(3/4) (Kleiber). WBE 1997 derives 3/4 from space-filling fractal supply networks. Disputed: Dodds 2001 found 2/3 fits small masses; Kolokotrones 2010 (Nature 464:753) found curvature, local exponent rising 0.57 to 0.87, so not a pure power law; a finite-size analysis says WBE gives 3/4 only for infinitely large organisms. I did not find WBE's reply. [search: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08920 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.3659] Cost: one pow(). Clade: a fine *balance curve* for cost vs size (bigger = cheaper per gram), but don't present it as a law.
- **Neural cellular automata** (Mordvintsev, Randazzo, Niklasson, Levin; Distill, 11 Feb 2020). One small learned update rule per cell, 16 continuous channels; trained by gradient descent with a sample pool; models trained with damage regrow. [search: https://distill.pub/2020/selforg/ ; DOI 10.23915/distill.00023] Parameter count of the original: not found (an isotropic follow-up reports 9,408). Training needs GPU/autodiff; runtime inference per cell is cheap. Clade: interesting only. Can't train in browser during play; baked sprites would not be heritable.
- **CPPN and HyperNEAT** (Stanley 2007; HyperNEAT 2009). A small network maps (x, y, distance...) to colour or weight; composed functions give symmetry, repetition and variation. Picbreeder (people interactively evolving CPPN images, no goal) and quality-diversity methods built on it. [search: https://www.heatonresearch.com/encog/neat/hyperneat_structure.html ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.05143] Cost: a few dozen weights, evaluated per pixel at birth. Clade: a mutable, heritable coat/shape function: child = parent with small weight noise, so family resemblance is automatic. Pixel art at 16-32 px is trivial cost.
- **Karl Sims' virtual creatures** (SIGGRAPH 1994). Body and brain both encoded as recurrent directed graphs; fitness for swimming, walking, jumping, following; run on a CM-5. Population "several hundred" per his page; exact size/hours not verified. [search: https://karlsims.com/evolved-virtual-creatures.html] Cost: heavy (physics). Clade: nothing to reuse except the idea that a node can repeat (repeat-count gene).
- **Lenia and extensions.** You know Lenia. Flow-Lenia adds mass conservation and puts update-rule parameters into the flowing mass, so different "species" coexist and compete in one world (ALIFE 2023 best paper; Artificial Life 31(2), 2025); a demo ran 144 creatures in 1024x1024 for 200k steps. [search: https://research.google/pubs/flow-lenia-towards-open-ended-evolution-in-cellular-automata-through-mass-conservation-and-parameter-localization/ ; https://sites.google.com/view/flowlenia/] Cost: FFT convolutions per frame; GPU in practice. Clade: not for the garden sim (8 h in 5 s); at most a visual for the sea board. Blunt: strong visuals, weak legibility, no tier list.
- **Levin: bioelectric morphogenesis.** Brief gap-junction block or innexin RNAi gives two-headed planaria that stay two-headed through re-cutting in plain water; resetting via H+/K+-ATPase is reported. "Pattern memory" in voltage is a hypothesis; I found no independent peer-reviewed replication or critique in this search. [search: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5753428/figure/F4 ; https://now.tufts.edu/2011/01/28/heads-or-tails-cells-electricity-decides] Clade: interesting only (a "regrow lost limb" flavour at best).

## 4. Artificial life and open-ended evolution

- **Tierra** (Ray 1991). Self-replicating programs compete for CPU time and memory. Produced parasites, hosts immune to them. Complexity growth was not seen: Standish measured no growth; Ray moved to Network Tierra for more room. [search: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_(computer_simulation) ; https://www.tomray.me/pubs/fatm/node49.html] Clade: parasites-as-ecology is the lesson, not the code.
- **Avida** (Ofria, Adami, Lenski). Digital organisms earn energy by performing logic functions. Lenski et al., Nature 423:139 (2003): complex functions (many coordinated instructions) evolve via chains of simpler functions. Caveat: the rewarded functions were set by the experimenter. [search: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2003/05/030508075843.htm ; https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse573/04au/Messages/paper1/0006.html] Clade: shows why "stepping stones" must pay off individually; supports the plateau-and-jump pacing.
- **Polyworld** (Yaeger). Neural-net creatures evolving freely by natural selection with emergent behaviours. [search: Wikipedia Tierra search result set; no detailed read]. Clade: none.
- **Fontana's AlChemy (algorithmic chemistry, 1994).** Lambda-calculus expressions react with each other; self-maintaining "organisations" appear. 2024 replication with far more compute (Mathis, Patel, Weimer, Forrest; Chaos): organisations emerge more often than expected and resist collapse, but cannot easily be combined into higher-order ones. [search: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12137v2] Clade: interesting only.
- **Kauffman autocatalytic sets; Hordijk-Steel RAF.** An RAF set is a reaction subset that is self-sustaining from a food set and collectively autocatalytic. Likely to exist in simple polymer models, but much smaller than the original giant-component picture; the maximal RAF is computed in polynomial time, the smallest is NP-hard. [search: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6315399 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.03953] Clade: no use unless the game gets a chemistry layer.
- **Assembly theory** (Cronin, Walker). Counts the minimum number of join steps to build an object, multiplied by copy number, as a signature of selection. Criticism: Uthamacumaran, Abrahao, Kiani, Zenil (npj Syst Biol Appl 10:82, 2024) argue the assembly index is an encoding used by standard compressors and add that it does not explain selection or evolution; Ozelim et al. 2024 claim a proof of equivalence to Shannon entropy/LZ compression; Cronin replied that the benchmarks misclassify the data. Ongoing dispute. [search: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00901 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15108v7 ; https://comdig.cssociety.org/2024/08/20/on-the-salient-limitations-of-the-methods-of-assembly-theory-and-their-classification-of-molecular-biosignatures-2/] I did not open Cronin's own papers. Clade: avoid.
- **Open-endedness metrics.** MODES toolbox (Dolson, Vostinar, Wiser, Ofria; Artificial Life 25(1), 2019): four hallmarks, change, novelty, complexity, ecological potential; C++ code; tested on NK landscapes and Avida; treats open-endedness as a continuum. Exact formulas not read. [search: https://peerj.com/preprints/27249v2.html] Bedau-Packard evolutionary-activity statistics are the older ancestor. [mem] Clade: a *dev-panel* diagnostic ("is the tier list still turning over, still producing novel body kinds?") is cheap and fits "balance by feel, a simple curve model and the dev panel".
- **Games.** Spore, Species ALRE, Thrive, Equilinox, Niche, Real Evolution are in `diverse-evolution-games.md`, `real-evolution.md`, `garden-models.md`. Added here:
  - *Creatures* (1996, Steve Grand): each Norn has a neural network for senses/behaviour and an artificial biochemistry with hormone-like signals; early commercial genetic-level artificial life. EMMA award 1996; sales about 400,000 by Feb 1998 or 500,000 by Q2 1998 (sources disagree); the PlayStation port scored 43/100 on Metacritic. [src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creatures_(1996_video_game) via search] Lesson (my reading): the depth was loved by a niche; the shallow port flopped. Cost is high per creature; Clade's few active creatures could afford a tiny version, but the hours-of-garden sim cannot.
  - *The Bibites* (Leo Caussan): neural-net brains and genes that evolve; speciation comes in bursts; old irrelevant species are pruned from the record to save data; players can compare species' genes and brains. Full version via Patreon, free demo. I could not verify the evolution algorithm (NEAT or not). [search: https://thebibites.itch.io/the-bibites/devlog/809631/the-bibites-060-organs-and-science ; https://the-bibites.fandom.com/wiki/Bibites] Clade: "prune old species from the visible record" is a cheap tree-readability rule.
  - Pattern across all of them: emergent systems deliver surprise but weak legibility; authored systems deliver reads. Models that map to a single visible axis (niche model, morphospace map) keep legibility.

## 5. Whole-cell and agent-based approaches, briefly
- **Karr et al. 2012 (Cell).** *Mycoplasma genitalium*, 525 genes, 28 submodels each run on a 1 s step and integrated; 192 wild-type cells and 3,011 single-gene deletants run on a 128-core cluster (MATLAB); gene-essentiality predicted with 79% accuracy; metabolite, flux and RNA levels qualitatively agree. [search: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3413483 ; https://courses.cs.duke.edu/spring15/compsci662/pdf/karr.2012.pdf] Runtime per cell not verified here.
- **Thornburg et al. 2022 (Cell).** JCVI-syn3A, 493 genes, whole-cell kinetic model from cryo-tomography geometry; hybrid stochastic/deterministic; simulating a 120-min cycle takes about 4 h in one source; predicted that restoring two non-essential pyruvate dehydrogenase genes would shorten division by about 13%, and the lab saw about 12% (120 to 105 min). [search: https://www.jcvi.org/publications/fundamental-behaviors-emerge-simulations-living-minimal-cell] The "4D" and GPU details are not confirmed.
- **Where they fall short (my synthesis, not from one source):** smallest known cells only; inputs from the literature and other organisms; hours of compute per cell cycle; no development, no multicellularity, no evolution. Agent-based ecology/evolution models (Avida, Polyworld, Bibites) capture selection dynamics but fitness is whatever the designer set, the circularity point raised against Wolfram's model too.
- Clade: avoid; two to six orders of magnitude too costly and no player-visible payoff.

## Ranked shortlist for Clade (all *idea*; the user owns the design)
1. *idea* **Hidden drift, then breakthrough, for lineage change.** Each lineage banks invisible neutral mutations per egg; a new body option or tier appears only when drift plus a trigger pass a threshold, with some forms reachable only via stepping stones. Source pattern: Wolfram plateaus/jumps, neutral networks, Avida stepping stones. Cost: a counter and a Poisson draw per lineage; catch-up is a single draw.
2. *idea* **Niche-model food web for the garden.** Each species gets a niche value and a prey interval; predation links and omnivory come out of two numbers (S, connectance). Cost: O(S^2) once per species set; legible as a bar on one axis.
3. *idea* **Body as a few continuous shape genes (Raup/Thompson).** 2-3 genes warp the skeleton and part positions, each tied to a stat (cost-size curve like M^0.75, speed, armour). A codex map shows "forms seen / not yet". Pair with a repeat-count gene for Limbs/Sides. Cost: near zero.
4. *idea* **Adaptive-dynamics branching as the speciation rule.** A crowded middle of a niche's resource axis lets a lineage split in two; invasion fitness sign decides. Cost: a few lines per niche per generation; closed enough to jump in catch-up.
5. *idea* **Heritable coat patterns from a tiny CPPN or a Gierer-Meinhardt grid with 2-3 genes.** Children look like parents by construction; patterns bake into sprites at birth. Cost: a few thousand ops per creature.

## What I would avoid, and why
- Running Wolfram-style CA evolution at runtime: about 10^9 updates per organism (my estimate), and the forms have no limbs or mouths.
- Whole-cell, assembly theory, AlChemy, RAF sets: huge cost or contested science, and nothing the player can see.
- NCA/HyperNEAT/Lenia training or per-frame inference in the garden sim: GPU or training pipeline, unreadable output; not compatible with 8 h in 5 s.
- NK landscapes as the main fitness: random epistasis is illegible; authored synergies read better.
- Presenting Kleiber's 3/4 or "polyplexy" as facts: the exponent is disputed, the term is Wolfram's own.
