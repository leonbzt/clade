# Eras and tree: Cambrian to life ashore (R3)

Researched 4 Nov 2026. Sources are mostly Wikipedia pages (which cite the primary papers; named in the bullets) plus two journal pages opened directly. The fetch budget ran out before I could open more primary papers, so items marked **[unverified]** are textbook background or search hits I did not open. Dates are rough (+/- several Myr, ICS 2024 boundaries); causes of extinctions are debated.

## Summary
- Eight eras fit the real record, from the Ediacaran-Cambrian turnover to coal forests. Each has a clear "new piece" and a reason to move on.
- Oxygen is the most-cited slow driver but the least certain. One model has O2 about 2-11% through the Cambrian and Ordovician, a Devonian rise, and 25-30% in the Carboniferous. Charcoal and halite proxies disagree on the Carboniferous.
- Two big drivers beat oxygen as era triggers: (1) extinction filters (end-Botomian, end-Ordovician, Kellwasser/Hangenberg) that remove the old dominant group; (2) land plants, which change CO2, soil and nutrient runoff and so feed back to the sea.
- Reefs change builders every era: archaeocyaths, then sponges and microbes, then bryozoan/stromatoporoid/coral, then coral-stromatoporoid, then sponge/microbe after the Devonian crash.
- The "Cambrian substrate revolution" (mats to mixed sediment) is real in trace fossils but its oxygen effect is now questioned (Cribb et al. 2023).
- Life ashore was gradual: spores about 470 Ma (molecular clocks say earlier), vascular plants about 430 Ma, millipedes about 425 Ma, tetrapod-like tracks about 395 Ma (disputed), amniotes in the late Carboniferous.
- Fish and plants arrive as pieces: jawed fishes (Silurian) and trees (Devonian) are the best "chess piece" upgrades.

## Details (by era)

### Cambrian (1-2)
- Start about 538.8 Ma. The explosion lasted about 13-25 Myr, with the burst fading around 521 Ma (first trilobites). Disparity stayed low until after the Ordovician radiation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_explosion
- Triggers are debated: oxygen, ocean calcium and alkalinity, predation, development genes, snowball ice ages. No single winner; some say it is no faster than other radiations ("push of the past"). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_explosion
- O2 was about 3-14% in the Cambrian and a stepwise model (GEOCARBSULFOR) gives 2-11% from Cambrian to mid-Ordovician with a transient rise during the explosion; burrowing may have damped it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian and https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06383-y (Krause et al. 2018, opened). Model is very sensitive to the carbon isotope record.
- Substrate revolution: mats gave way to burrowed mixed sediment, from Ediacaran traces about 565 Ma through the Cambrian. Proposed by Seilacher and Pfluger 1994 and Bottjer, Hagadorn and Dornbos 2000 (https://rock.geosociety.org/gsatoday/archive/10/9/article/i1052-5173-10-9-1.htm). Doubts: some Ediacaran "burrows" may not be animals; giant protists make similar traces; Cribb et al. 2023 say bioturbation did not oxygenate shallow sediment much. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_substrate_revolution
- Archaeocyaths built the first large animal reefs, with calcimicrobes; they went extinct in the Sinsk event (about 513-508, End-Botomian, the first Phanerozoic mass extinction) and lithistid sponges took over. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian
- Kalkarindji flood basalts (from about 511 Ma) added CO2 and warmed the sea. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian

### Ordovician (3-4)
- GOBE: 497-467 Ma by one 2020 study (Fan et al., Science), not one event; families roughly tripled; Cambrian and Ordovician radiations may be one continuum. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Ordovician_Biodiversification_Event
- Hypotheses (no consensus): cooling (conodont thermometry, Trotter et al. 2008), oxygenation (thallium isotopes; Edwards et al. 2017: O2 rise tracks diversity, correlation not proof), nutrients from orogeny, ecological escalation, asteroid breakup (dates to about 467.5, later than the burst). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41561-017-0006-3 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Ordovician_Biodiversification_Event
- Plankton: acritarch diversity peaks mid-Ordovician; trilobites and cephalopods entered the water column. Same page.
- Late Ordovician mass extinction: second largest. 49-60% of genera, about 85% of species (Christie et al. 2013). Pulse 1 at glaciation (cold, falling sea level), pulse 2 with anoxia and euxinia. Trilobites lost about 70% of genera; Cambrian-type groups never recovered. Causes disputed (glaciation cause, volcanism warming or cooling, whether anoxia began early). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician%E2%80%93Silurian_extinction_events

### Silurian and Devonian (4-7)
- Silurian was once seen as stable and warm; isotope records show many swings. CO2 about 4,500 ppm. Reefs patchy. Photosymbiotic corals in the Middle Silurian. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silurian
- Jawed fish diversify in the Silurian as jawless fish decline (Brazeau and Friedman 2015). Eurypterids abundant in shallow seas. Same page.
- Cooksonia (Wenlock) is the oldest known vascular plant, near rivers; Baragwanathia (about 420) hints at a longer history. Millipedes from about 425 Ma (Kerrera), but the age of Cowie Formation is disputed. Same page.
- Land plant origin dates conflict. Molecular-clock studies put it far earlier than fossil spores (Morris et al. 2018 PNAS, "The timescale of early land plant evolution", ~Cambrian-Ordovician; I did not open it) https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-timescale-of-early-land-plant-evolution/ **[unverified]**. Existing overview already gives ~500 Ma.
- Devonian: warm early, mid-Devonian cooling by about 5 C linked to forest carbon burial (Joachimski et al. 2009), O2 "unusually low" in Givetian/Frasnian, which Clack (2007) ties to air-breathing in lobe-finned fish. Cladoxylopsid trees formed the first forests (Eifelian); Archaeopteris followed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian
- Krause et al. 2018 model: O2 near modern in the Devonian but a trough about 14.5% at about 393 Ma. Relation to the first forests is modelled, not measured. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-06383-y
- Ammonoids first about 400 Ma. "Devonian Nekton Revolution" (Klug et al. 2010) is questioned (Whalen and Briggs 2018 found no significant overall nekton increase). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian
- Late Devonian extinction: Kellwasser about 372 Ma (two pulses), Hangenberg about 359 Ma. Hit reef builders (stromatoporoids, rugose and tabulate corals; reefs not back to Middle Devonian extent until the Mesozoic), trilobite orders, placoderms (half of families in Kellwasser, rest in Hangenberg). Marine only. Causes: anoxia is best supported; plant-driven nutrient runoff, cooling, volcanism, impacts all proposed; severity estimates range from 22% of families to 75%+ of species. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Devonian_mass_extinction
- Tetrapods: Tungsenia about 409, Panderichthys about 380, Tiktaalik-like elpistostegids vanish at the F-F boundary. Zachelmie trackways (Poland, about 395 Ma, Niedzwiedzki et al. 2010) predate body fossils by about 18 Myr, but are disputed (Lucas 2015 reads them as fish feeding traces). Many limb traits arose in water; Acanthostega was aquatic (Clack). First tetrapods lived in shallow, oxygen-poor coastal water. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_tetrapods and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian

### Carboniferous (8)
- Most coal formed in the late Carboniferous and early Permian. Two explanations: slow lignin-degrading fungi, or humid geography and subsiding basins (Nelsen et al. 2016). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous
- O2 from about 15-20% to 25-30% (Mills 2023), but charcoal says above 21% and halite inclusions say about 15% (Brand 2021). Oxygen's role in arthropod size is an open debate. Same page.
- Late Paleozoic Ice Age from about 335 to 290 Ma. Rainforest collapse in the Kasimovian when CO2 fell below about 400 ppm; lycopsid forests lost, replaced by other forest types (Lucas 2023: replacement, not a total loss). Same page.
- Amniotes appear in the late Carboniferous; the page gives no details on the earliest fossils.

## Which real changes could nudge a free-evolving garden
Real drivers, ranked by how well they map to a sim (a judgement of mine, not a sourced claim):
- **Plants first**: Land plants and trees lower CO2, add soil and runoff; the Devonian chain (trees, nutrients, algae blooms, anoxia, extinction) is a feedback the sim can run (hypothesis only, "conjectural" per the Wikipedia page).
- **Extinction as the filter**: each era boundary except the first removes the old dominant group (archaeocyaths, trilobites, placoderms, tabulate/rugose corals).
- **Oxygen as a floor**: bigger, more active animals need more O2. Treat as a soft gate not a clock; the numbers are model-dependent.
- **New habitat kinds**: mats to burrowed mud; planktonic water column; shallow coastal swamps; floodplains. Fit "new habitat kinds" in big-picture.md.
- **Predation arms race**: escalation is proposed for the Cambrian and GOBE but predator ratios did not track diversification rates in one test (Cambrian explosion page).

## Contradictions and cautions for big-picture.md
- "Oxygen ... does the nudging" is mixed: oxygen is plausible but poorly constrained; extinction filters and plant effects are better supported.
- "Plants are the baseline": in a Cambrian sea there are no land plants. Plants (spores) come about 470 Ma at the earliest fossil dates; mats and algae are the baseline there.
- The substrate shift is real but not clearly oxygen-driven.
- "Real era" boundaries are fuzzy: Cambrian-Ordovician radiation may be one continuum, and nekton revolution is disputed.

## Ideas for Clade
- **idea**: Make the era-end an extinction event that removes the apex group's slot and lets the best-adapted line from the garden (not the player's) win it, like trilobite to nautiloid to placoderm.
- **idea**: Give each era one hidden slow variable (plants on land, oxygen, ice) that the garden's own outcomes push, with the real date window as a fallback timer.
- **idea**: Let reef builders swap each era (archaeocyath, sponge, bryozoan/coral, coral-stromatoporoid, sponge/microbe) as the habitat that "resets".
