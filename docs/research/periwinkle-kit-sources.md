**Grade:** ✓ = read on the cited page today. ~ = known biology, the page cited is where to check it; not read today.

## Summary
- A periwinkle's body maps cleanly onto the kit's six places: snout and radula (front), tentacles and eyes (head),
  the foot (limbs), the mantle cavity and gill (sides), the shell (back), the operculum on the rear of the foot (tail).
- The genus is unusually rich in **real, recent, measured evolution**: live birth evolved in *L. saxatilis* within
  about 100,000 years; crab and wave ecotypes evolved in parallel on many shores; shells thicken within one life when
  crabs are near. Each is a ready-made "this happened" card.
- Three reproductive modes in three common species make a natural spawn axis: drifting egg capsules (*littorea*),
  jelly egg masses on wrack (*obtusata*), a brood pouch with crawl-away young (*saxatilis*).
- Weak spots: no source read on mucus-trail following in *Littorina* itself (only *Echinolittorina*), none on the
  operculum's sealing time, none on predation numbers by crabs or dog whelks.

## Parts by place (real names; simplest first where biology gives an order)
| Place | Real structures and names | What it does | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front | **snout** (not retractile, so not a proboscis); **radula** in a **radular sac**, **taenioglossan** (7 teeth a row); **odontophore** (the tongue cartilage that drives it) | scrapes film algae off rock | ✓ [Fox], ✓ [Krings 2022] |
| Head | two **cephalic tentacles**, banded black in *littorea*; one **eye** at each tentacle's base; **osphradium** (a chemical sense patch in the mantle cavity) | sight, smell of water | ✓ [Fox], ✓ [MarLIN] |
| Limbs | the **foot** and its **sole**; **pedal mucus**; in *saxatilis* the wave ecotype has a **wider aperture for a bigger foot** that resists dislodging | crawling on a mucus film, clinging | ✓ [Fox], ✓ [Wiki *saxatilis*] |
| Sides | **mantle cavity** with a **monopectinate ctenidium** (gill, filaments on one side); **hypobranchial gland** (mucus) | breathing; high-shore littorinids breathe air in a damp mantle cavity | ✓ [Fox]; air ~ [SeaLifeBase family] |
| Back | the **shell**: conical, 5–6 whorls (*littorea*); **ridged and banded** (crab ecotype) vs **smooth, thin** (wave ecotype); **thickened shell** induced by crab scent | armour vs crabs and whelks, water store | ✓ [Wiki *saxatilis*], ✓ [Trussell & Smith 2000] |
| Tail | the **operculum**, a horny disc on the rear of the foot that shuts the aperture; a **dried mucus seal** round the aperture in air | closes the door against drying and attack | ✓ [Fox], ✓ [MarLIN] |

Inner organs a trait could name: **digestive gland**, **style sac with a protostyle** (a churning rod), **capsule
gland** and **albumen gland** (make egg capsules), **pallial oviduct**, **penis** (behind the right tentacle) ✓ [Fox].

## Traits and behaviours (no place)
- **Brood pouch / live birth** (*L. saxatilis*): "a brood pouch with non-planktonic shelled embryos" ✓; up to a few
  hundred embryos at mixed stages; evolved in about the last 100,000 years from small changes at about 50 genome
  regions; the young cut their own way out ✓ [Stankowski et al. 2024, via Science News].
- **Drifting egg capsules** (*littorea*): capsules about 1 mm with 2–3 eggs (up to 9); 10,000–100,000 eggs a year;
  released on spring tides, Feb–June; larvae drift 11–30 days, up to six weeks, more than 10 km ✓ [MarLIN].
- **Egg masses on wrack** (*obtusata*): internal fertilisation, masses of up to 280 eggs laid on seaweed ✓ [Wildlife Trusts].
- **Induced thick shell**: snails in water carrying crab scent thicken their shells faster than controls, matching the
  shell change seen since the invading green crab arrived ✓ [Trussell & Smith 2000]. A trait that grows on its own
  when a hunter is near.
- **Ecotypes**: crab ecotype (ridged, banded, robust, small aperture against drying, high shore on barnacles) and wave
  ecotype (smooth, thin, wide aperture, big foot, low shore on mussels); both meet mid-shore ✓ [Wiki *saxatilis*].
  Parallel origins on many shores ~ [Johannesson, see Wiki refs].
- **Mucus seal and clustering in air**: inactive when exposed, seals the aperture with dried mucus, gathers in pools
  and clusters to cut water loss ✓ [MarLIN].
- **Heat behaviours** (*Echinolittorina*, high-shore littorinids): standing and **towering** (males stack on females)
  take over 35% of time in the hot season; crevices and aggregations over 70% in the cool season; **trail
  following** switches from mate search to shelter with the tide ✓ [Ng et al. 2021].
- **Seaweed mimicry** (*obtusata*): olive and yellow forms look like bladderwrack's air bladders ✓ [Wildlife Trusts].
- **Long life, slow start**: 5–10 years, mature at 2–3 years; grows 0.065–0.097 mm a day ✓ [MarLIN].

## Enemies and costs (for the board, not the kit)
- **Trematodes** (*Cryptocotyle lingua*, *Himasthla*, *Renicola*) may sterilise the snail ✓ [MarLIN]: a real "parasite
  castrator", a cost a hunter does not explain.
- Crabs and dog whelks are the hunters the shell answers ~ [Wiki *saxatilis*; Wiki *Nucella*].
