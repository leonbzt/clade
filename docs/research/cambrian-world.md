# R2: The Cambrian shallow sea (and what the toys got right or wrong)

Researcher pass, 4 Nov 2026. **Evidence tags:** **[V]** = page read this session. **[K]** = from my background
knowledge; the URL is the page to check, but **I did not fetch it** (the web-fetch budget ran out mid-pass). Treat [K]

## Summary
- "Cambrian" spans ~539 to ~485 Ma. The famous faunas are not one world: archaeocyath reefs (~525 to ~510 Ma), Chengjiang
  (~518 Ma), Emu Bay Shale (~515 Ma), Burgess Shale (~506 to 508 Ma). The toys mix them. Tag boards "composite Cambrian".
- **The Burgess Shale animals were not (all) living where they were buried and the site was deeper than the reef.** The toy's
  mud-flat `real:` line is wrong. The Burgess "reef" was an algal escarpment, not an archaeocyath reef.
- Cambrian food webs look modern in shape (Dunne 2008) but herbivory was rare: Wiwaxia was the only possible herbivore they
  found in the Burgess web. Real base: phytoplankton, suspended and benthic detritus, bacteria.
- Radiodonts: Hurdia and Peytoia (hurdiids) were probably sediment sifters, not hunters. The "16,000 lenses" eye belongs to
  Emu Bay "Anomalocaris", now *Echidnacaris*, only distantly related to true *Anomalocaris*.
- Mats are real but shrinking (substrate revolution). Dead-water basins are real as episodes, but animals did not live in them.
- Volcanic islands: **I found no direct Cambrian island-fauna study.** Altai-Sayan island-arc terranes hold archaeocyath
  limestones (titles only, [K]). Island ecology here is inference.

## Details

### Settings and habitats
- **Archaeocyath reefs:** archaeocyaths are the first reef-building animals on Wikipedia's account, first in Siberia ~525 Ma,
  declining from ~516 Ma, nearly gone by the Middle Cambrian; consensus growing that they were sponges; reefs up to ~10 m
  deep structure in warm shallow seas. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeocyatha
- Early reefs were mostly calcareous algae (Renalcis, Epiphyton), with archaeocyaths rich only in the later Early Cambrian;
  associated: echinoderms, hyoliths, brachiopods, trilobites, sponges, cavity dwellers, borings (Trypanites). [V]
  James & Debrenne 1980, Acta Palaeontol. Pol. 25: https://app.pan.pl/archive/published/app25/app25-655.pdf
- Earlier "reefs" of Cloudina (Ediacaran) exist, so "first big animal reefs" is fair, "first reefs" is not. [K]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudina
- **Burgess Shale setting:** organisms lived on mud banks against the outside of an algal reef escarpment hundreds of metres
  high; slumps and turbidity currents carried some of them downslope into deeper water ("Many of the associated fauna were
  caught up in these slumps and were transported with the mud"). [V] https://hoopermuseum.carleton.ca/burgessshale/formation.html
  (Preservation mechanism: Gaines et al. 2012, PNAS [K], https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1111784109.)
- **Emu Bay Shale** (S. Australia, Cambrian Stage 4, ~515 Ma): source of the big radiodont eyes. [V]
  https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/39add97c-9c30-41eb-ab2d-dd2efa0fcb45
- **Chengjiang** (~520 Ma) and **Burgess** (505 Ma by Dunne et al.; sources vary 505 to 508): shallow vs deep depth of
  each is debated; I could not verify numbers. [uncertain]
- **Shallow siliciclastic seafloors:** trace fossils dominate (Skolithos "pipe rock" = dense vertical tubes of suspension-feeding
  worms; Cruziana = arthropod furrows; Rusophycus = resting traces). Real shallow-shelf Cambrian is mostly known from these,
  not from soft-bodied fossils. [K] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skolithos , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruziana_(trace_fossil)
- **Oxygen:** Cambrian ocean oxygen was low and patchy; shelf euxinia is documented later in the period (Gill et al. 2011,
  Nature 469) and Sperling et al. 2013 (PNAS) argue thresholds for animal ecology were met but modest. [K]
  https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09700 , https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1303904110
- **Substrate revolution:** mats formed a barrier over anoxic sulfidic sediment; burrowers broke them; mats survived mostly in
  hypersaline lagoons, brackish estuaries, rock surfaces and the deep sea; process long, patchy, at different rates; the
  mat-miner community died out by the mid-Cambrian. Concept: Bottjer, Hagadorn & Dornbos 2000 (GSA Today 10:9);
  Seilacher & Pflüger 1994. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_substrate_revolution
  Caveat from the same page: a 2023 study found bioturbation did not extensively oxygenate shallow sediments across the
  Ediacaran to Cambrian boundary.
- **Volcanic islands:** search hits show Late Neoproterozoic to Cambrian island-arc and oceanic complexes in Gorny Altai
  with carbonate buildups and Atdabanian archaeocyath/trilobite/small-shelly faunas in the Altai-Sayan fold area [titles
  only, unread]: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331066306 ,
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242174035 . Which of those buildups sat on volcanic edifices I could not confirm.
  Also Shackleton Limestone reefs (Transantarctic Mts): https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227758453 (unread).
  **Honest status: a volcanic-island Cambrian reef is plausible and partly supported, its food web is unknown.**

### Food webs and trophic roles
- Chengjiang: 85 taxa, 559 links (33 trophic species, 99 links). Burgess: 142 taxa, 771 links (48 trophic species, 249
  links). Both resemble modern webs in scale-dependent structure; mean trophic level 2.84 / 2.72. [V]
  Dunne et al. 2008, PLoS Biol: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2689700
- Assumed base: algae, phytoplankton, bacterioplankton, suspended organic matter, benthic detritus, zooplankton. Predators
  include *Anomalocaris* and *Ottoia* (gut contents link Ottoia to the hyolith *Haplophrentis*). "Only possible herbivore in
  the Burgess Shale was *Wiwaxia*." Feeding is mostly inferred; 60% (Chengjiang) and 37% (Burgess) of trophic-species
  links are low-certainty. [V] Same paper.
- Hurdiid radiodonts (Hurdia, Peytoia, *Cambroraster*) show filtering/sediment-sifting appendages; Cambroraster
  was described as a sediment sieve exploiting infaunal food (Moysiuk & Caron 2019, Proc R Soc B). [K]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambroraster , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurdiidae
- *Anomalocaris canadensis* anatomy (Daley & Edgecombe 2015, J Paleontol 88): small oval carapace with paired stalked eyes,
  triradial oral cone, frontal appendages narrower than once drawn, midgut with paired glands. [V]
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-paleontology/article/morphology-of-anomalocaris-canadensis-from-the-burgess-shale/9B9BA3A9BF0037D7D22358DA17CF44AC
  Its oral cone is "not a classic Peytoia" (Daley & Bergström 2012) and probably could not crush mineralised shells. [K]
- *Echidnacaris briggsi* (the Emu Bay eye owner): moved out of *Anomalocaris* in 2023; largest eyes >3 cm, >13,000 lenses
  (original paper: at least 16,000); possibly a suspension feeder; ~33 to 49 cm long. [V]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echidnacaris

## Corrections: toys/life2-data.ts, life2-habitats.ts, garden-data.ts
Format: quote, problem, fix, source. S = substantive.

1. **S** life2-habitats mud: `real: 'Mudflows buried the Burgess Shale animals where they lived.'` Partly wrong: some lived on mud banks by an algal reef; slumps transported them into deeper water. Fix: `'Mud slumps swept animals from banks beside a reef cliff into deeper water and buried them.'` Hooper Museum [V].
2. **S** life2-habitats reef: `'Sponge-like archaeocyaths and microbes built the first big animal reefs.'` Mostly fine (sponge affinity "growing consensus", calcimicrobes). Add: they were gone by mid-Cambrian, so Burgess animals (Wiwaxia, Anomalocaris, Peytoia, Hurdia) did not share these reefs. Fix: call the habitat "composite Cambrian" or use Series 2 reef fauna. Archaeocyatha page [V].
3. **S** life2-data compound: `'Anomalocaris had over 16,000 lenses per eye.'` The count is from Emu Bay Shale radiodont eyes, now *Echidnacaris* (distantly related); "at least 16,000" in 2011, >13,000 in later counts. Fix: `'A giant Cambrian radiodont had eyes with about 13,000 to 16,000 lenses.'` [V] Paterson 2011 (Adelaide record) and Echidnacaris page.
4. **S** garden-data: `peytoia ... 'A radiodont, relative of Anomalocaris.'`, role predator, and `hurdia ... role: 'predator'`. Hurdiids probably sediment sifters; their "predator" role is weak. Fix: role grazer/burrower-type "sifter", or mark L confidence. Moysiuk & Caron 2019 [K]; Daley et al. 2009 Science 323 [K].
5. **S** habitats `predator: 'Peytoia'/'Hurdia'` (mud, basin) follows from 4; keep Anomalocaris/Echidnacaris-like for hunters, or use Sidneyia/Ottoia as the carnivores.
6. **S** life2-data NODES.mat: `'Mats carpeted the early seafloor.'` True of the Ediacaran; by the Cambrian mats were retreating. Fix: `'Mats carpeted Ediacaran seafloors and faded as burrowers spread, lasting longest in restricted settings.'` Wikipedia/Bottjer 2000 [V].
7. garden-data anomalocaris: `'up to a metre long or so.'` Likely high. Fix: `'roughly 40 cm for the Burgess animal; the largest radiodonts were much later.'` [K, medium]; Echidnacaris estimate 33 to 49 cm [V].
8. life2-data oxygen/gill: `'Oxygen limited Cambrian body size.'` Contested. Fix: `'Low and patchy oxygen may have limited some animals.'` Sperling et al. 2013 [K].
9. life2-data claw: `'Sidneyia crushed shelled prey.'` Supported by gut contents; "could" is safer. Fix: `'Sidneyia ate shelled prey: its gut held trilobites, hyoliths and ostracods.'` [K]
10. life2-data hook: `'Ottoia guts held shelled hyoliths.'` Correct (Haplophrentis [V]). But the part's effect "Opens brachiopod beds" has no direct record; Ottoia ate hyoliths and other worms. Fix: add a hyolith node or call the node "shell bed". [V]
11. life2-data PLANS.worm blurb: `'In mud it tunnels'`, `real: 'Like Ottoia, a burrowing Burgess Shale worm.'` Fine. Ottoia lived in burrows [K].
12. life2-data chaetae: `'Burgessochaeta had bristled sides.'` Fine; but garden-data says Burgessochaeta fed on sediment in mud. Diet and burrowing are uncertain. Fix: `'A bristled worm; how it fed is not known.'` [K, low].
13. life2-data sclerite: `'Palaeoscolecid worms wore small plates.'` Correct (phosphatic sclerites). [K]
14. life2-data digger: `'Cruziana trails record trilobites digging.'` Cruziana = shallow furrowing/feeding by arthropods, mostly trilobites. Fix: `'Cruziana trails record trilobites furrowing through the sediment.'` [K]
15. life2-data enrol: `'Trilobites rolled into a ball.'` Many did, but tight enrolment became common after the Cambrian; early Cambrian trilobites often could not. Fix: `'Some trilobites rolled up; the habit grew common after the Cambrian.'` [K, medium]
16. life2-data spine: `'Trilobite spines.'` Fine.
17. life2-data pouch: `'Waptia carried its eggs.'` Correct: brood care in *Waptia*, Caron & Vannier 2016, Curr Biol. [K]
18. life2-data gut: `'Leanchoilia had branching gut glands.'` Correct (Butterfield 2002). [K]
19. life2-data blood: `'Many arthropods carry oxygen on copper-based haemocyanin.'` True of living crustaceans, chelicerates, myriapods; Cambrian blood is unknown, and haemocyanin does not make low oxygen free. Fix: say "game rule, not fossil fact". [K]
20. life2-data symbiont: `'Some living worms host sulfur-eating bacteria.'` True (e.g. *Olavius algarvensis*, Dubilier et al.). No Cambrian evidence known to me. Label as modern analogue. [K]
21. life2-data deep: `'Treptichnus burrows mark the start of the Cambrian.'` True: *T. pedum* defines the base (Fortune Head). Maker unknown; early burrows shallow, so a "Deep burrow" is a stretch. [K] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treptichnus
22. garden-data scenella: `'A small cap-shaped shell, probably a grazer.'` and basin placement. Identity debated and it is found on hard parts/sponges; no basin evidence. Fix: `'A small cap-shaped shell of uncertain relationship; diet unknown.'` and move it to the reef. [K, low]

## Contradictions with big-picture.md
- "Shallow sea around the island" vs the Burgess fauna: deeper-water, partly transported. Not a contradiction if boards are composite.
- §6.5 burrowers churning mats away is supported by the substrate revolution. But "mat grazer" tiers lean on a thin real base: herbivory was rare.
- Dead-water basin with residents: fossil record has episodic anoxia, but no resident animals in it.

## Ideas for Clade
- **idea:** Make the reef the Ediacaran/Series 2 algae-archaeocyath reef, and keep the Burgess mud as a *deeper* slope board where slumps (not storms) cause the "mudslide".
- **idea:** Add hyoliths (*Haplophrentis*) as the documented prey node; they are the best-attested Cambrian prey.
- **idea:** Add "carbonate mound on volcanic rock" as the island's reef board, since archaeocyath limestones do occur in island-arc terranes.
