## Summary
- **Urchins fill five of six places and leave the tail empty.** Front = Aristotle's lantern (underneath), head =
  no head (light sense spread over the body, or the diadematid "eyes"), limbs = tube feet, sides = spines and
  pedicellariae, back = the test with the periproct on top. A radial plan needs the kit to allow an empty place
  (PR #3 says the same).
- **Urchins have the clearest real build split in the roster:** grazer that bores a home pit in rock (purple) vs
  long venomous spines and a shadow alarm (*Diadema*). Two Lives, two builds, same clade.
- **Shore crabs fill all six places with real, named parts** and have the richest either/or: a right crusher and a
  left cutter claw, and the green-vs-red moult choice (moult often and stay weak but tolerant, or delay and become a
  strong red brute). That second one maps onto the `moult` mechanic almost word for word.

## Urchins: traits and behaviours (no place)
- **Rock boring** (purple urchin): teeth and spines scrape a pit; spines wear down and regrow; a young urchin can dig
  a hole it later cannot leave and stays for life ✓ [ADW purpuratus]. *Paracentrotus lividus* bores soft rock with
  its jaws ✓ [Wiki Sea urchin]. A natural "home" trait: safe but stuck.
- **Venomous spines and a shadow alarm** (*Diadema*): thin, hollow, brittle spines with a mild poison in the mucus;
  very light-sensitive, points its spines at a shadow and retreats ✓ [ADW Diadema].
- **Night grazing, day shelter**, gathering in groups (up to 20 per m² in the open) ✓ [ADW Diadema].
- **Kelp eater**: purple urchins prefer giant kelp and can strip whole kelp forests; eaten by sea otters and sea
  stars (incl. *Pycnopodia*) ✓ [ADW purpuratus]. A board fact: urchin barrens.
- **Spawn**: separate sexes, eggs and sperm to the water; *Diadema* spawns monthly in summer ✓; larva the
  **echinopluteus** with 12 ciliated arms, 4–6 weeks (*Diadema*) ✓ [ADW Diadema] or months ✓ [Wiki]; the juvenile
  grows as a **rudiment** on the larva's left side and metamorphosis can take an hour ✓ [Wiki Sea urchin]. A strong
  "grow a new body inside the old one" card.
- **Long life, no moult**: purple urchins mature at about 2 years (~25 mm) and live about 20 ✓; red urchins may reach
  about 200 ✓ [Wiki Sea urchin, ADW purpuratus].
- **Covering** (holding shells and weed over the test with tube feet) ~ [check: Wiki Sea urchin says "carry or
  shelter among objects" only].

## Urchins: enemies and costs (for the board)
- The 1983 Caribbean die-off killed up to 97% of adult *Diadema* in some areas, likely a water-borne pathogen ✓
  [ADW Diadema]; another die-off in 2022 ~ [CBS, phys.org, not read].
- Predators: triggerfish, spiny lobsters, helmet snails (*Diadema*) ✓; otters and sea stars (purple) ✓.

## Shore crabs: traits and behaviours (no place)
- **Green or red: the moult choice.** Crabs that delay moulting turn red, are stronger and more aggressive, but stand
  low salt and low oxygen worse ✓ [Wiki Carcinus]. A real trade-off for the `moult` mechanic: moult often (grow,
  tolerant, soft) or hold the shell (strong, fragile to bad water).
- **Bivalve crusher**: mainly clams, mussels and young oysters; up to 40 small clams (about 13 mm) a day; adult
  oyster shells are too tough ✓. Also worms, small crustaceans, smaller crabs, eelgrass seeds ✓ [Wiki Carcinus]. The
  `beats: ['shell']` line is well founded. Periwinkles as prey: see Trussell & Smith 2000 in the periwinkle page.
- **Hardy**: salt 4–52‰, water 0–30 °C ✓ [Wiki Carcinus]. A tolerance trait with real numbers.
- **Brood**: females make more than 400,000 eggs; larvae develop offshore through several stages to a **megalopa**,
  which moults into a crab on the shore ✓; zoea stages (four, ~) and days not on the page.
- **Tide clock**: active by day but set by the tide, any hour ✓ [Wiki Carcinus].
- **Autotomy** (dropping a leg or claw to escape) ~; claw loss lowers mating success ~ [Abby-Kalio & Warner, title only].

## Shore crabs: enemies and costs (for the board)
- **Sacculina carcini**, a parasitic barnacle that takes over the crab's body ✓ named [Wiki Carcinus]; that it
  castrates and feminises the host ~. The crab twin of the periwinkle's trematodes.
- Blue crabs and native rock crabs hold it back in America ✓ [Wiki Carcinus].
