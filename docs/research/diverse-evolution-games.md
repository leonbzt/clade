# Diverse evolution in games: what creates (or kills) the felt sense of divergence

Researched 3 Nov 2026. Question: how do evolution games make lineages feel different, and why do some feel samey?

## Summary
- Evidence is strongest where players complained: Species ALRE and Spore (traits that do nothing), Ecosystem (random, hard to read).
- Games that feel diverse make a trait change what the creature *does* (a new verb), not just a number.
- Authored synergy and counter (Evolution board game, Plague Inc) gives combos and reads clearly; emergent physics (Ecosystem, Species) gives surprise but often mush.
- Trade-offs and a shared point budget (Thrive MP, Niche, Evolution) force a "this lineage, not that one" choice.
- Diversity is felt through pressure (niches, predators, counters) and through a visible record (trees, reports).
- Thin or secondhand: Hatched (no such game found), idle-evolution player feedback (only store pages), designer talks on emergent vs authored (nothing solid).

## Per game

**The Sapling** (solo dev Wessel Stoop; Steam 88% of ~1,970 reviews, accessed Nov 2026)
- Model: no DNA. Organisms are body parts; each part may mutate in offspring at a player-set rate; a dice roll picks the part and change (colour, size, morphology); animals also get instincts and random hormones. [Kinesis](https://kinesismagazine.com/2025/06/06/simulating-evolution-from-one-humble-sapling/)
- The player designs species by hand; random mutation is an optional sandbox mode. Steam text: species fill niches, some are outcompeted, and "when a food source evolves a defense, a different clade can move into the vacant niche". [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/997380/The_Sapling/)
- Divergence is shown through the ecosystem (who survives, who takes a vacated niche), not through a trait list.
- Reception: "rough around the edges but solid foundation" ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2019/12/the-sapling-a-plant-and-animal-design-and-evolution-sim-is-now-in-early-access/)). I found no actual player review text, so no complaint evidence either way.

**Equilinox** (ThinMatrix; ~93% positive, ~3,000 reviews)
- "Evolution" is an unlock tree, not selection. You evolve existing species into new ones (lizard to frog to toad, needed near a plant to evolve the next). Each step takes time and needs conditions held throughout. Diversity Points buy species and "genetic modification" of traits. [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2018/11/relaxing-nature-sim-equilinox-is-out-seems-to-be-a-hit-with-users), [fan-wiki summary via TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Equilinox)
- A commenter argued it is not an evolution sim because you place everything. Opinion, but it is a useful lens: variety comes from authored content, not divergence.
- Good fit for "each unlock is a noticeable step"; zero lineage divergence.

**Thrive** (open source, microbe stage)
- Each editor visit gives 100 MP; adding or removing organelles costs MP; removing one placed this session refunds. Organelles have clear jobs (mitochondria ATP, chloroplast sugar from light, flagella speed, cilia turning, lysosome digests walls, nucleus doubles size and halves damage but is expensive). The nucleus gates the other membrane organelles. Wiki says each visit is 50 million years (a search snippet said 100 million, so the sources disagree). [Thrive wiki](https://wiki.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/wiki/Help_And_Tips)
- Auto-evo evolves competing species every editor visit and reports who evolved, died or changed population. Patches (habitats) are separate arenas; you can move members between them. [same wiki](https://wiki.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/wiki/Help_And_Tips)
- Diversity limits, by devs: auto-evo merges near-identical species to save compute; a small change like adding cytoplasm may not split a species, a new organelle type or nucleus does. [forum](https://community.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/t/auto-evo-algorithm/9065?page=3), [search digest, secondhand]
- Player note (1.0.1 thread): it is faster to "slap a bunch of cells and go macroscopic" than to spend MP on specialised cell types. That is a dominant-strategy / skipped-diversity complaint. [thread](https://community.revolutionarygamesstudio.com/t/1-0-1-general-feedback-thread/8944)

**Species: Artificial Life, Real Evolution** (Quasar; ~74% positive, ~1,000 reviews)
- Emergent: creatures defined by genes, offspring randomly modified, environment selects. [Steam data mirror](https://steamdata.ai/en-US/game/774541/species-artificial-life-real-evolution)
- Clearest "samey" evidence found, from one Steam discussion post: creatures "grow stubs and usually become bipeds with extra floating limbs"; you can't tell an aquatic herbivore from a land carnivore; legs don't improve speed or survival; one dominant herbivore/omnivore spreads over zones and blocks new species; carnivores survive only in narrow conditions; selection "seems arbitrary". [thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/774541/discussions/0/3075370124707956701)
- Rebuttal in the same thread: evolution has no goal and worms are realistic. The poster's reply: players expect *visible* adaptation (fins, teeth, side eyes) and traits that clearly affect survival.
- One player's post, not a survey. The failure mode it names (traits with no legible survival effect, one winner) matches other games.

**Niche: a genetics survival game** (Stray Fawn)
- Each body slot (ears, horns, eyes, head...) has a gene pair, top dominant and bottom recessive; genes shape skills (walking, swimming, flying, foraging, strength, fertility). Over 100 genes. Mutation is random, but you may pick one wanted mutation per animal; offspring only get a chance of it. Players act as sexual selection. [Naked Scientists interview](https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/198834), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niche_(video_game))
- Trade-offs: gaining one ability can cost another; strong genes can still give long-term infertility. Two identical immunity genes shorten life (inbreeding). A new start carries over the gene pool and mutation options. [Keengamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/niche-a-genetics-survival-game-review/)
- Praise: realistic core, "never completely SOL" thanks to mutations. Complaints: breeding is a dice roll, recessive traits are hard to remove, Cubed3 "as a game, I didn't care for it". [OpenCritic list](https://opencritic.com/game/10099/niche-a-genetics-survival-game), Keengamer. Nintendo World Report called it "generic in totality" (one critic, via Wikipedia).
- Divergence is shown in bloodlines and a physical look per animal. It is mostly learn-by-luck, not designed combos.

**Spore** (Maxis)
- Creature editor: parts carry costs and abilities set by the parts chosen; mouth, limbs, hands and feet drive the stage-2 abilities; only eyes give sight and the other sensors are cosmetic. [Spore wiki](https://spore.fandom.com/wiki/Creature_Creator)
- Criticism (forum posts, not critics, so treat as opinion): body shape/placement "didn't matter"; a reply says part values did matter but only the highest-value part counted (no stacking). Wright himself kept tweaking looks after each evolution. [Straight Dope thread](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/spore-2008/915281), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/opinion-is-i-spore-i-for-everyone-)
- Critics: creature creation and evolution praised, gameplay on top "very simplistic" (Wired), "really repetitive" (1UP). [Critical reception](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/critical-reception-ea-maxis-i-spore-i-)
- Lesson: a rich editor that produces endless *looks* but few *distinct play consequences* feels diverse for minutes and samey after.
- Hecker's "My Liner Notes for Spore" would be the primary dev source but the site was down when fetched; I did not read it.

**Evolution (North Star board game)**
- Each species: body size, population, up to 3 trait cards (2 in two-player); 129 trait cards (17 carnivore, 7 of every other trait); about 12,000 possible species per The Guardian. A species that does not eat enough loses population; extinct species refund cards equal to its trait count. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_(board_game)), [rules](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0283/4324/files/Evolution-Rules-Web_1.pdf)
- Combos are authored and relational: carnivore attacks others; some traits defend, carnivores can hold counters; fat tissue stores food (only trait allowed twice) and lets a fed predator keep attacking; foraging/fat tissue protect against starvation. [rules summary via search](https://www.theboardgamefamily.com/2015/04/evolution-board-game-review/)
- Designer origin: Dominic Crapuchettes with a Russian game by a Moscow biology professor. I found no source on why individual combos were chosen.
- Praise: "easy to learn, tactics get deep, best strategy depends on others" (Nature, via Wikipedia). Divergence is shown on the table: each species is a visibly different stack of cards.

**Plague Inc** (Ndemic)
- DNA Points buy Transmissions, Symptoms, Abilities. Disease types change the rules: Virus mutates fast, Fungus is slow and needs spore abilities, Prion is slow and brain-based, Nano-Virus starts cure research at once, Bio-Weapon lethality rises over time, special plagues have own win conditions. Authored combos: Neural Atrophy plus Genetic Hardening; Symbiosis cuts severity not lethality. [wiki](https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/Beginner_Guide)
- Guides stress staging: spread first, delay visible symptoms. Pressure is a visible counter (cure research, detection), so choices have reasons.
- Divergence comes from *rule-changing archetypes* plus a legible opposing force, not from many small stat perks.

**Creatures** (Steve Grand)
- Haploid genome, simulated biochemistry, neural-net brain. Mutation can disable organs or give odd traits (blind, immortal via efficient digestion). Each first-game copy ships with unique starting eggs. Players bred for colour and immune strength, swapped Norns, and told stories of surprising changes. Dawkins praised it. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creatures_(video_game_series))
- Divergence is *personal*: individual creatures with names and histories. Not a design for balance.

**Ecosystem** (Slug Disco; Steam Mostly Positive ~595)
- Synthetic DNA encodes skeleton, brain and combat abilities in a physics ocean; swimming emerges (flailing, then competent in ~10 generations). Player tools can force traits (tentacles, size, part count); population charts and phylogenetic trees track species. [Slug Disco](https://slugdisco.com/ecosystem-game-info/)
- Complaints (aggregator summary, secondhand): evolution "appears random and doesn't lead to meaningful adaptations", little agency, "too many factors", changes should show by generation 10, clunky editor, dysfunctional or monstrous mutants. [vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1133120/ecosystem.md)
- Praise: relaxing, watching creatures adapt, more diverse behaviours. [same]

**Hatched**: no game by that name found. Closest hits not researched further (Strange Seed: DNA from kills unlocks body parts; Voidling Bound: branching evolutions). [search]

**Idle / incremental**
- *Evolve* (Demagorgon, browser): species give a built-in trait and belong to a genus; the custom-race lab (after the first T4 reset) lets you build a race from unlocked traits. Traits must be unlocked by resetting as the stock species that owns them, or via a genus-mate. Own-genus traits cost 1 gene less, opposing genus 1 more. [r/EvolveIdle guide summary, secondhand](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/EvolveIdle/comments/1klyzkt/guide_the_new_custom_lab) (page itself returned 403, so details come from a search digest).
- Evolve's species wiki table could not be fetched; trait-count claims are unverified.
- Mobile "evolution" idles (Evolution Galaxy, Game of Evolution) are merge or tap ladders; store pages only, no player feedback on sameness found.

## Synthesis: mechanisms that reliably create a felt sense of diverse evolution
1. **Verbs, not numbers.** A trait unlocks or removes an action (carnivore attack, flagellum movement, Plague's Fungus needing spores). Pure +% perks read as the same build.
2. **Costs from one shared budget.** MP, body size/food in Evolution, fertility in Niche, nucleus upkeep in Thrive. Every gain is a visible loss.
3. **Relational fitness: counters and pressure.** A trait is strong *against* something (carnivore vs defences, predators in Thrive, cure in Plague Inc, Sapling vacated niches). This makes lineages specialise in different directions.
4. **Authored synergy pairs, few and legible.** Fat tissue + carnivore, Neural Atrophy + Genetic Hardening. Players can name the combo and tell others.
5. **Rule-changing archetypes at the root** (Plague disease types, Evolve genus, Thrive nucleus gating). Branches then differ in how they play, not just stats.
6. **Visible morphology tied to function.** Swimming evolving in Ecosystem, Spore parts changing the body. Players want fins for water, teeth for carnivores (Species thread).
7. **A readable lineage record**: phylogenetic trees, auto-evo reports, named bloodlines (Creatures, Niche, Ecosystem, Thrive).
8. **Carry-over / heritage**: Niche passes the gene pool and mutation options; Evolve unlocks traits across resets. Each run is a step, not a restart.

## Pitfalls
1. **Cosmetic or non-binding traits** (Spore sensors, "legs don't improve speed" in Species). Players detect this within hours.
2. **Randomness without a legible cause** (Ecosystem, Niche breeding, Species "arbitrary" selection). Surprise needs an explanation after the fact.
3. **One dominant generalist or strategy** (Species herbivore, Thrive "slap cells"). Selection converges unless niches or counters punish it.
4. **Unlock trees mistaken for divergence** (Equilinox): fine for progression, but everyone ends up with the same species list.
5. **Too many factors to read** (Ecosystem). Diversity must be traceable to a cause, or it feels like noise.

## Ideas for Clade
- **idea**: Give each gene a verb (changes what the body *does* or what a niche rewards) plus a cost on a shared 9-pip budget; keep pure stat genes out.
- **idea**: A small set of 6-10 named, authored gene pairs with a visible combo badge and a counter niche (like Fat tissue + Carnivore), shown in the tier list and the clade tree.
- **idea**: Show lineage divergence on the clade screen as a diff of body and stats against the parent, plus "this run changed X".
