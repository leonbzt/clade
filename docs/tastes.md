# Your tastes, wishes and lessons

**Yours.** What you like, want and learned, carried over from v1 (vision, decisions and playtests, 25 Oct – 2 Nov)
so the rebuild keeps it. AI condensation, in your words where possible; nothing added. Fix anything, and say when a
line no longer holds. The game itself is defined by the spec (`spec.md`, after the toy); this file is the taste it
has to fit.

## The feeling
- **Growing an ecosystem and growing your species** is the most important thing. A garden you tend; an interesting,
  rewarding ecosystem.
- **"Discovering life yourself"** (1 Nov). **Author the laws of nature, not the history** (1 Nov).
- **"Always adapt to your surroundings"** (2 Nov).
- **The side view and "the feeling you grow everything"** (2 Nov).
- **"It feels alive"** and the gram amounts everywhere (11e playtest).
- **"The 'living' ecosystem is also really important to me to have the feeling"** (4 Nov).
- **The big picture "sounds really cool",** and the example of a booming worm changing the place "is just what i had in mind" (4 Nov).
- **"I like to be the little creature and chase things around"** (4 Nov). You want to feel "really a part of that habitat".
- **"World is core"** (5 Nov). "Species has to change the board." The "feeling of really adapting and having maybe a recognizable
  species is good."

## How it should play
- **"A lot of feel with simple mechanics that can get done, instead of overly complicated and not fitting
  things"** (26 Oct).
- **Simple core, strong progression.** The core can be simple and repetitive if progression and choosing what to do
  are very strong. Hand-curated progression; shortcuts to proven concepts (IdleOn, Idle Obelisk Miner, Melvor, OSRS).
- **Idle / incremental** (2 Nov). Hybrid idle: early systems need attention, then go idle. Fast unlocks at the start
  to learn, then a midgame where the real game starts.
- **Things push back.** Progression is clear and decisions matter; you choose where to put resources but are never
  locked out. Pressure is good: newcomers compete with the species already in a niche.
- **Builds like game characters:** "like levelling a specific pure OSRS character, or making a Dark Souls build"
  (3 Nov). All-rounders are bad; there is always pressure ("damage") and armour, or a locked reward.
- **"Fast climb means noticeable improvement"** (3 Nov). The speed of M1 was right.
- **Adapting has to feel like adapting.** Buying every trait isn't it. Not every stat can be improved: some choices
  trade ("more spread while drying out more", 26 Oct).
- **Tiers like a game tier list** (S, A, B…), not bronze/gold or stars (26 Oct). Niches × tiers with "some light
  ladder feeling" (2 Nov).
- **Lineages are your characters;** you grow one thing the whole time (29 Oct). The first lineage is a plant (1 Nov).
  The game starts at home, at the vent.
- Breeding yes, but scarce. Stakes: species die out and the game goes on.
- **Swings are wanted** (grazers that eat plants to death, species dying out). Chains are features: tune them, don't
  cut them. Balance by feel; abstract multipliers are fine; feeling right beats everything being readable.
- **Readable:** show why something is winning; important news stands out.
- **"Better fewer time savings and better map and game"** (5 Nov): a richer world is worth slower catch-up.
- **The climb:** "skill tree is cool, and i like the climb" (5 Nov). You like diverse verbs and playstyles "a lot".
- **"Simple and impactful" decisions, budgets and moving for rewards:** "a great little minigame to solve" (4 Nov). "In this simple
  setup its easy to introduce lots of different mechanics."
- **Rare drops, the most efficient route, milestones you worked for, getting mechanically better at bosses** (4 Nov), from Idle
  Obelisk Miner and Dark Souls; a build where many things stack into one strong attack; IdleOn-style placing of effects.
- **Tier talk is the voice of the game:** "no upkeep in mud was a gamechanger for the worm and brought it to s tier. i think this is
  exactly the way someone should talk about the game" (4 Nov).

## Evolution and life
- **Genes appear and spread by themselves**, and that stays at the heart. Mutations can be an unexpected reward.
  Choices sit on top.
- You liked watching variants, and **the gene pips "like a chromosome arm"** (27 Oct).
- **The body determines the stats** (2 Nov): genome → body → stats → niche.
- Rarity uses game-like tiers; founding helps noticeably; extremes stay rare. New species start fresh (2 Nov).
- **A connected tree** that loosely follows the real tree of life, with a lot of freedom. Modern forms first; the
  deep-time road later. A basal ancestor (an early land plant) branching into liverworts and mosses. Lichens are an
  unlock. Groups are named base species (a slug you found is still a slug).
- Later: partnership doors, new diets through genes.
- **Growth forms play differently:** "moss vs herb vs tree should all play different" (3 Nov). More kingdoms
  and species.

## The world
- **A young volcanic island**, subtropical at home; progression follows **ecological succession**. Tile types change
  over generations: lava → bare ground → forest floor.
- **Contrast between areas** (the wind side). Landmarks: vents, springs, beaches, caves.
- **Habitats the sim detects, in detail**, small patches too (2 Nov: "I like getting the habitats in detail").
  Habitats filled like an RPG equipment loadout. Open slots are good.
- **Patches "really governed", where booms and crashes happen;** species spread, with "only few named rules". The overview map "is
  very important for feeling" (5 Nov). "Not all roles should fit everywhere" (5 Nov).
- **The sim has a life of its own;** you can't influence everything. Calm means slow, big stores. Fewer visible
  layers, more hidden depth.
- **Real units:** grams (g → kg → t), 10 m tiles.
- **Animals:** small, cute individuals roaming the garden, something to discover; you see why things happen.
  Snails, springtails, spiders; later beetles, bees, maybe seabirds and lizards; bats yes; more cute animals.
  Earthworms out. Animals releasing nitrogen matters.
- Later: trees and fruits; geology (leaning yes); hiding in shade and caves; the sea line and life coming ashore;
  outside inputs onto the island. A long target: the island's climax closes an era.

## Methods and mechanics you like (26 Oct)
- **Sim methods:** cellular automata, forest fire, L-systems, space colonization, physarum agents, boids, ant
  pheromone trails. Multiple grid layers (readable, with toggles).
- **Mechanics:** territory capture (Go, Risk), tile placement (Carcassonne), connect the flow, hidden info
  (Minesweeper-like puzzles), a timing bar in some small place, a skill you build up, draft and deckbuilding
  ("has to be done very well"), auto battlers ("set up and see how good it does"), idle skilling.
- Relationships and guilds changing the rules. A colony and a garden at two zoom levels. Many small games that
  show the diversity of life.

## Lessons from playing
- **Toys (26 Oct):** specimen: "there isn't a lot to decide, you can't shape the plant a lot, it grows random";
  patch: "the feeling of directing it doesn't feel strong"; rules: "the decisions still feel a little weak".
- **Slice 01 (26 Oct):** you wanted the minigame of growing shoots, leaves and roots back.
- **11e (1 Nov):** snails and springtails overran the map (too many drawn); one moss and one liverwort took
  everything; nitrogen looked noisy.
- **12a (2 Nov):** "a little lost and it was random"; "everything still feels really unclear"; "I don't get what
  the levels benefit".
- **Grow toy, first build (2 Nov):** "fun in principle"; managing the growth and roots and directing the plant "is
  something I can imagine going forward with"; generations felt too short; the habitat should be shaped by its
  neighbours and its habitat type.
- **Grow toy pass 2 (3 Nov):** herb and tree "play differently"; you liked the tree's look, the spring leaves and
  cutting in winter; the tree "grew to little"; "still not a good feeling game", maybe less autogrowth.
- **Playtest 02 (3 Nov):** liked the attributes, pressures and genome upkeep; "I just auto every time" (pulses);
  "still seems like a toy somehow, not like a serious approach that's well thought through."
- **The stand, part 1a (3 Nov):** the shade "makes everything a lot more readable"; growing the saplings "still not
  really fun", "maybe three at once is too much"; "more puzzle style with tiles or settler or something strategic";
  "a solid foundation but i don't like it as a final product"
- **Cradle of Life (4 Nov):** tangled systems and a new idea pulling away ended it. You liked the core and the RPG body doll with
  rarity materials and genes, but bare menus gave "no real fun or sense of improvement or pull".
- **Toys A and C (4 Nov):** clear limits felt good, "a little bit like a drafting game"; threats "felt really cool"; placing pieces
  "wasn't fun yet and felt clunky".
- **The Life (4 Nov):** you started new rounds without deciding to. Rare mutations: "i like it a lot this way, and felt really
  compelled to go on".
- **The living garden toy (5 Nov):** no big pull to wait, but "really felt sad i couldn't play to fill the other niches" and "really wanted to play as a predator". Liked the creatures roaming, the overview, the
  bars, skipping to see changes, variant offers. "i don't really recognize the creatures... that can come later."
- **The M1 run (5 Nov):** "the basics seem fun and as imagined." Liked the board layout, strategy from moves, foods and skills, picking
  species and slots, the compact UI, the hunter's life. "The map ust looks dead"; "no real progression"; "the looks are okay for a start
  but i want more work on that." Wants to see adaptations on the body and build it visually.
- **The sea toy (5 Nov):** "really cool and is similar to what i've imagined"; "feel really nice to see the changes." The patch graph "looks like
  population crashes." Species shown only as numbers are "a little confusing". Wants the small drawn interactions to play out, "like something was hunted", later.
- **The goal (5 Nov):** "a clean freshly put together full version that i can release to get feedback and ideas": a good look, recognisable species, one full run, different playstyles,
  and the link from "i played this run and advanced my lineage" to seeing it on the map, to "what run do i pick next?"
- **Putting it together (6 Nov):** "i liked the individual game, and i like the lviing sea, and i want to put it together."
- **The family tree after a Life (6 Nov):** "sounds really good"; "watch it spread is also a core aspect." You like the classic skill tree and the pure family tree, but a family tree "could get too crowded".
- **Growing and mutating (6 Nov):** "you actually grow up and grow the body parts to use them"; "i really like the random genes and \"mutation\" aspect"; lineage "should also offer verbs and mechanics and stats"; finding genes "makes more sense for reproduction".
- **Your animal, drawn (6 Nov):** "the animal or creature you see and build is qctually the one drawn fells important, and also see your impact." You like recipes and a strong generator together.
- **Every animal playable (6 Nov):** "you can play as every lineage"; "generalized bodys and creation"; "not author tons of other creatures that you can't play." Lineages change "because you played them well and picked adaptations that formed a real new lineage. that also looks differnt."
- **Creature art (6 Nov):** code-drawn low-poly is "the most robust and fitting"; you also like code-drawn pixels and hand-drawn bases "for good recognizability".
- **Something to arrive at (6 Nov):** a named real species as "an end point for a lineage", "pretty strong", with "a slightly different sidebuild that could be more optimal under some circumstances"; "it would be cool to discover an own thing". A new lineage should feel like "a more direct or \"branded\" result that feels like an upgrade". Beware bodies that stack "to much" or "can't be changed again".
- **Food (6 Nov):** "a living food layer is definetley the direction i want to move, maybe multilayered animal food chain first too."
- **The project (1–2 Nov):** AI iteration made it convoluted; long sessions from sim runs and tests; the vision
  drifted; parts were half-baked.
