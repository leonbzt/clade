# Critical review: 25 Oct 2026

A one-time review of the concept (your ideas and the AI proposals) from a game design and development point of view.
It's reference, not a decision log.

## Verdict

**The core fantasy is strong and unusual:** raise species like characters, and watch them build a living garden.
There's a real audience where idle, RPG, cozy and biology overlap; Cell to Singularity, Equilinox and Wingspan
each prove a slice of it.

**The danger is not the idea but its width.** As described, this is a multi-year game for a team. It becomes
achievable only if almost everything shares one core and the first version is ruthlessly small.

## Risks, ranked

| #   | Risk                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | How to handle it                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Scope.** Parallel slots × several minigames × map sim × tree of life × gene pool × garden × bundles × skills. Spore and Thrive are the cautionary tales: distinct minigames per stage felt shallow and took years.            | One shared adaptation core; one activity minigame **per kingdom**, not per guild; an MVP of 1 slot and 1 kingdom (systems S18). Every new system has to earn its place after a playtest. |
| 2   | **Ecosystem sim vs. idle maths.** Emergent sims boom and crash, which is bad for offline progress and for "never die out". Per-plant simulation is also expensive on phones.                                                     | Simulate abstractly (niche shares, logistic growth, multipliers) and render richly (systems S9).                                                                                          |
| 3   | **Overload from parallel minigames.** IdleOn proves parallel characters work, and is also famous for overwhelming players.                                                                                                      | Slots unlock slowly; every activity minigame uses the same UI pattern; automation arrives before the next slot does.                                                                      |
| 4   | **Pleasing four audiences pleases none.** Biology, idle, RPG and cozy want different things.                                                                                                                                     | Pick a primary audience: **idle/RPG players who like nature**. Biology is the content and flavour source (real clades, facts, real trade-offs), not complex mechanics. Cozy is the tone. |
| 5   | **Animals cost far more art than plants.** Animals need movement and animation; plants can be procedural and static-ish.                                                                                                         | Keep animals small, seen in groups, with simple procedural motion. Plants carry the visual richness.                                                                                      |
| 6   | **Balancing by feel alone fails for long curves.** You can't play-test a 20-hour idle curve by feel.                                                                                                                             | No bot, but a **simple curve model** (a spreadsheet or a 50-line script: "how long does stage N take idle?") plus a dev panel with time skip. Feel tunes it; the model keeps it sane.     |
| 7   | **Biology accuracy, and AI making facts up.** Wrong facts in front of biology fans hurt credibility.                                                                                                                             | Every codex fact gets a source link. Research is done by an agent that cites, and you spot-check.                                                                                         |
| 8   | **"Hard to die out" vs. "push back".** Without death, where's the tension?                                                                                                                                                       | Push back through opportunity cost (slots, time, resources), niche saturation, competition for *share*, seasons and bundle requirements. Not through death.                              |
| 9   | **Prestige timing degenerates** (always release at X).                                                                                                                                                                           | Diminishing returns in a niche, plus a projected-gain display, plus reasons to release early (bundles, seasons, establishment windows).                                                   |
| 10  | **Mobile performance and battery** with a living landscape.                                                                                                                                                                       | Render only when visible; cap the frame rate; pause when backgrounded; calculate offline progress on resume (cheap with the abstract model).                                             |

## Specific ideas

| Idea                                                          | Verdict                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Parallel species with release as per-slot prestige *(yours)*  | **Strong.** It fits idle play perfectly and gives a constant "release now or push?" decision. Risk: overload (risk 3).                                                          |
| Speciation follows the real tree *(yours)*                    | **Strong**, if nodes are reached through key innovations (systems S7), not stat thresholds. Otherwise it teaches biology fans a misconception.                                   |
| Guilds that mix kingdoms *(yours)*                            | **Good, and accurate.** It does double the content matrix, so keep guilds per kingdom few at first.                                                                              |
| "Different mechanics for adapting" per group *(yours)*        | **Push back.** Different mechanics per *guild* is the most expensive idea in the whole design. One per *kingdom* at most, with guilds varying the rules.                       |
| Abstract multipliers over readability *(yours)*               | **Fine for idle**, but anchor them in visible causes ("pollinators: flowers ×1.5"). Otherwise the garden stops feeling alive, and feel is your top goal.                       |
| Gene pool screen *(yours)*                                    | **Good,** if every visit holds a decision (systems S6). Passive stats screens get ignored.                                                                                       |
| Feel-only balancing *(yours)*                                 | **Partly.** See risk 6.                                                                                                                                                         |
| Island + succession world *(AI)*                              | **Strong fit**, but it's a world-building commitment. Decide before writing content.                                                                                             |
| Bundles, relationship hearts, seasons from Stardew *(AI)*     | **Bundles: strong** (they solve horizontal resources). **Hearts: good, cheap.** **Seasons: good, but only once the core works.**                                               |
| Founder colony with dispersal *(AI, systems S4 option C)*      | **The best answer to "where do I found vs. release",** but it needs a very clear fit preview. Option D is the fallback if C confuses playtesters.                                |
| Tending moments *(AI)*                                        | **Nice to have.** Defer.                                                                                                                                                        |

## Cut or defer from version 1

- Eras and resets
- Seasons
- Tending moments
- Relationship hearts
- A second kingdom
- More than 1 slot
- Off-tree speculative species
- The full codex

**Version 1 is:** one plant species, from founding to release into a small garden, done so well it feels magical.
